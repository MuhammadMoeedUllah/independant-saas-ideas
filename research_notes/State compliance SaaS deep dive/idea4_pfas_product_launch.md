## Q1. Testing science: methods, thresholds referenced by law, cost/turnaround, component-level validity, certificate reuse/lot validity

### Takeaway
The working industry pattern is a **total-fluorine / total-organic-fluorine screen by combustion ion chromatography (CIC)**, with targeted LC-MS/MS (EN 17681-1:2025 for textiles) used only to confirm positives. California and Vermont set TOF thresholds (100 ppm, falling to 50 ppm on 2027-01-01). Washington presumes intentional addition above 50 ppm total fluorine. Maine, Minnesota, New York, New Mexico and Connecticut use an "intentionally added" standard that has no numeric test threshold. A screen costs roughly €250–350 / US$300–500 per sample, and a lab report takes about 3–4 weeks. Component-level ("homogeneous material") testing has strong precedents that support reuse: CPSC 16 CFR 1109 component-part testing and the OEKO-TEX modular system. Both precedents also require **due care, documentation access, scope matching and periodic re-verification**, so a reusable certificate needs lot/version scoping and an expiry.

### Cited Findings

**Thresholds/methods referenced by states and standards**
- California AB 1817 (textiles): "regulated PFAS" means either PFAS intentionally added for a functional or technical effect, or PFAS present at ≥100 ppm measured as total organic fluorine from 2025-01-01, falling to ≥50 ppm from 2027-01-01. New (not previously used) textile articles containing regulated PFAS may not be made, sold or distributed in CA. — [Taft Law](https://taftlaw.com/news-events/law-bulletins/new-california-bill-restricts-pfas-content-in-textiles); [Bureau Veritas](https://www.cps.bureauveritas.com/newsroom/california-signs-pfas-textile-articles-bill-ab-1817)
- Law-firm summaries describe the AB 1817 threshold as applying to TOF "within the product or product components". If that wording holds, each component must pass on its own, and a finished-garment average does not satisfy the law. — summarized from [Morgan Lewis (Nov 2024)](https://morganlewis.com/pubs/2024/11/new-york-and-california-bans-on-pfas-in-textiles-and-apparel-begin-january-1-2025) and [Venable (Oct 2024)](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2024/10/fashion-industry-beware-pfas-bans-on-apparel). The exact phrase was not confirmed against H&SC §108970.
- AB 1817 scope: clothing, household fabrics, upholstery, towels, bedding. It excludes carpets/rugs, PFAS treatments for converted textiles/leather, and used articles. Certain PPE is exempt. Outdoor apparel for severe wet conditions may contain regulated PFAS until 2028-01-01 but must carry a "Made with PFAS chemicals" disclosure, including online. — [Taft Law](https://taftlaw.com/news-events/law-bulletins/new-california-bill-restricts-pfas-content-in-textiles); [TÜV SÜD](https://www.tuvsud.com/en-gb/e-ssentials-newsletter/consumer-products-and-retail-essentials/e-ssentials-14-2022/usa-california-restricts-pfas-in-textile-articles)
- Vermont: TOF threshold for apparel/textiles drops to 50 ppm by 2027, mirroring CA. Sources conflict on whether Vermont's apparel restriction began 2026-01-01 or 2026-07-01. — [HQTS](https://www.hqts.com/news/vermont-pfas-legislation); [Specialty Fabrics Review (Jun 2025)](https://specialtyfabricsreview.com/2025/06/25/vermont/)
- Washington (Safer Products, WAC 173-337 amendments adopted 2025-11-20, effective 2025-12-21): **total fluorine >50 ppm triggers a presumption that PFAS were intentionally added**. The draft had no threshold; the de minimis was added after comment. The presumption is rebuttable with a statement plus credible evidence that PFAS were not intentionally added. — [Akin Gump](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/blogs/pfas-press/washington-makes-a-de-minimis-change-to-its-pfas-restrictions); [SGS (Dec 2025)](https://www.sgs.com/en-ca/news/2025/12/safeguards-17425-washington-usa-regulates-pfas-in-consumer-goods). TF testing also catches inorganic fluorides, so non-PFAS products can be swept in until rebutted. — [Global ELR (Jan 2026)](https://www.globalelr.com/2026/01/washington-states-amended-pfas-rule-restricts-new-product-categories-and-adds-total-fluorine-threshold/)
- Minnesota reporting rule (published in State Register 2025-12-08): report when PFAS is intentionally added **or present as a contaminant above 100 ppm**. — [CDX summary](https://public.cdxsystem.com/web/cdx/w/minnesota-pfas-reporting-rules-finalized-ahead-of-2026-deadline)
- OEKO-TEX bans intentional PFAS use in textiles, leather, apparel and footwear across all certificates, but **"does NOT certify the product as 'PFAS-free'."** — [Hohenstein (OEKO-TEX institute)](https://www.hohenstein.de/en-us/oeko-tex/restrictions-and-testing/pfas)
- OEKO-TEX limit since 2024-01-01: 100 mg/kg (STANDARD 100, ECO PASSPORT, LEATHER STANDARD, ORGANIC COTTON). It switched from extractable organic fluorine to **total fluorine** to align with US TOF-based laws. — [Exponent (Feb 2024)](https://www.exponent.com/article/oeko-tex-reduces-pfas-limits-textiles-leather)
- OEKO-TEX itself notes there are "currently no industry standard testing methodologies for EOF, TOF, or total fluorine in textile and leather products." — [OEKO-TEX FAQ](https://oeko-tex.com/en/faq) (as summarized in search)
- A third-party blog says the 2026 OEKO-TEX update (effective June 1, 2026) cut the PFOS limit to 1 mg/kg and tightened exemptions for non-PFAS fluorine. **Unverified against OEKO-TEX.** — [Testex Textile blog](https://www.testextextile.com/oeko-tex-2026-new-regulations-take-effect-in-june-fail-to-clarify-these-5-changes-and-your-certification-could-be-instantly-invalidated/)
- ZDHC MRSL v3.0 (2022) banned all PFAS-based formulations for fashion, sport/outdoor apparel, footwear and home textiles, with a 12-month transition. — [Ecotextile (Sep 2022)](https://www.ecotextile.com/2022091545079/news/dyes-chemicals/pfas-to-be-restricted-in-zdhc-s-updated-mrsl/). Under MRSL 3.1, certifiers may test PFAS marker chemicals, screen total fluorine, then confirm. — [TexTalks](https://textalks.com/?p=84068). ZDHC planned an MRSL update and "comprehensive communication on PFAS" for **October 2026** (status as of 2026-10-09 unknown). — [Just Style](https://www.just-style.com/news/zdhc-pfas-textile-manufacturing/)
- France (EU benchmark, not US): Law 2025-188 (2025-02-27) bans PFAS in cosmetics, ski wax and textiles from January 2026, extending to all textiles by 2030. The draft decree proposed 25 ppb per individual PFAS (targeted), 250 ppb total targeted, and 50 ppm including polymers. Final figures not confirmed. — [SGS (Mar 2025)](https://www.sgs.com/en-ch/news/2025/03/safeguards-04025-france-bans-pfas-on-consumer-products); [UKFT](https://ukft.org/france-pfas-consumer-products)

**Test methods: what each shows**
- CIC (TOF/TF) burns the whole sample and converts fluorinated compounds to fluoride. It covers the bulk material, not only the surface, but it is destructive and **cannot distinguish PFAS from other fluorinated chemicals**. Total fluorine (TF) alone overstates PFAS because it includes inorganic F. — [Thermo Fisher app note (textiles)](https://lcms.labrulez.com/labrulez-bucket-strapi-h3hsga3/an_003571_ic_Integrion_fluoride_textiles_an003571_na_en_8643b1e42a.pdf); [HQTS PFAS testing guide](https://www.hqts.com/?p=183995)
- Thermo Fisher (vendor) argues targeted LC-MS captures only *extractable* PFAS, while TOF captures all organically bound F. That matters for textiles, where side-chain fluoropolymers are bound into fibers. — [Thermo Fisher AnalyteGuru blog](https://www.thermofisher.cn/blog/analyteguru/confident-total-organic-fluorine-analysis-in-textiles-with-the-cindion-combustion-ion-chromatography-system)
- In food-contact materials, extractable organic fluorine (EOF) was only a small fraction of total F, so targeted/EOF methods miss most fluorine (no textile equivalent found). — [LabRulez paper](https://lcms.labrulez.com/paper/28979)
- Targeted textile standard: **EN 17681-1:2025** (alkaline extract, LC-MS/MS) improves extraction of polymer-derived precursors and FTOHs. EN 17681-2 is the companion part (Shimadzu app note). — [LabRulez/Shimadzu](https://gcms.labrulez.com/paper/32936); [BSI listing](https://knowledge.bsigroup.com/products/textiles-and-textile-products-per-and-polyfluoroalkyl-substances-pfas-analysis-of-an-alkaline-extract-using-liquid-chromatography-and-tandem-mass-spectrometry)
- SGS offers a "Total Fluorine Screening" referencing **EN 14582** and recommends it for US markets where the substance list is not well defined. — [SGS TIC Mall: PFAS testing for clothing](https://ticmall.sgs.com/en/service_details/sl-pfas-testing-for-clothing-products)
- **EPA Method 1633** was validated for aqueous matrices (groundwater, surface water, leachate, wastewater), solids (soil, sediment, biosolids) and tissue. **Textiles and consumer products were not in the validation.** Lab textile work is a "modified/adapted 1633", reported as non-validated. — [DoD/EPA multi-lab validation study (CLU-IN)](https://clu-in.org:443/download/contaminantfocus/pfas/ER19-1409_Multi-Laboratory_Validation_Study_Report_(Volume I)_0.pdf); [ALS EnviroMail](https://www.alsglobal.com/en/News-and-publications/2023/07/Enviromail-22-USA-overcoming-EPA-1633)
- PIGE (particle-induced gamma-ray emission) is non-destructive and surface-weighted, and measures total F only. A Massachusetts turnout-gear study used 2×2 cm swatches with a 4.0 MeV proton beam for ~3 min. It reported 98–103% recovery, ±5.4% precision and an **MDL of 16.2 ppm F** in fabrics. — [Mass.gov turnout gear study](https://www.mass.gov/doc/pfas-in-turnout-gear-study/download). ANSTO's commercial PIGE service quotes detection down to 50 ppm. — [ANSTO](https://www.ansto.gov.au/news/nuclear-technique-supports-industry-led-action-plan-to-phase-out-packaging-pfas). The Peaslee group describes low-ppm sensitivity, good for intentionally coated papers and textiles. — [The Analytical Scientist](https://theanalyticalscientist.com/fields-applications/finger-lickin-fluoroalkyls)
- Academic two-step logic: rapid TF screen, then targeted validation. — [Notre Dame Peaslee Lab](https://sites.nd.edu/peasleelab/?p=318)
- US CPSC put out a solicitation for textile PFAS testing (TOF analysis followed by specific PFAS identification) at a firm fixed price: 5 samples with options up to 60. No unit price was shown. — [SAM listing via G2Xchange](https://g2xchange.com/app/opportunities/sam/8f8fe23b-9f30-4268-8fe2-bd3fb936ebdb)

**Cost and turnaround**
- Measurlabs (EU) TOF on paper, polymers and textiles costs **€250–350/sample** excluding VAT, plus a €97 per-order fee. It needs a 50 g sample, has a 5 mg/kg LOD, and offers batch discounts. — [Measurlabs](https://measurlabs.com/products/total-organic-fluorine-tof-content-in-paper)
- Scion (NZ) TOF costs NZ$1,100 for a single sample, NZ$420 each for 5, NZ$330 each for 11–30 and NZ$295 each above 30, with a 10 ppm LOD for solids. Volume discounts are steep, which matters for a network that batches tests. — [Scion](https://www.scionresearch.com/services/laboratory-services/pfas-testing-services)
- Wisconsin State Lab of Hygiene charges EPA 1633A (40 PFAS) at **$450 for water/tissue and $550 for solids** (date unclear; textile prep not included). — [WSLH](https://www.slh.wisc.edu/environmental/pfas)
- TESTEX (Swiss textile lab): targeted and non-targeted PFAS reports take **15–20 business days** from sample receipt. — [TESTEX factsheet](https://www.testex.com/uploads/files/downloads/TESTEX_Factsheet_PFAS_TS_EN.pdf)
- Draft 1633 turnaround was estimated at 4–5 weeks, against 2–3 weeks for 537-modified. — [ALS EnviroMail](https://www.alsglobal.com/en/News-and-publications/2023/07/Enviromail-22-USA-overcoming-EPA-1633)
- Non-target analysis costs more than target analysis and needs experienced LC-MS/MS technicians. — [HQTS](https://www.hqts.com/?p=183995)
- Capacity signals: SGS quadrupled North American PFAS testing capacity in June 2024 (environmental focus). — [SGS](https://www.sgs.com/en-tr/news/2024/06/sgs-quadruples-pfas-testing-capacity-in-north-america). Bureau Veritas markets a total-fluorine screen with "dramatically reduced turnaround" and a Buffalo lab with rush service. — [BV TF testing](https://www.cps.bureauveritas.com/needs/pfas-total-fluorine-testing); [BV Buffalo](https://www.cps.bureauveritas.com/needs/pfas-testing-bv-buffalo-lab)

**Component-level testing and certificate reuse precedents**
- **CPSC 16 CFR 1109** (closest US legal analog): a finished-product certifier may certify "based, in whole or in part, on passing component part test reports or certification" if conditions are met. Those conditions are: the supplier attests its testing complied (1109.5(g)(7)), the certifier attests it exercised **due care** (1109.5(g)(10)), and the certifier has access to the underlying test records. Hazards that are inherently finished-product (e.g., small parts) cannot be component-tested. — [CPSC Component Part Testing FAQ](https://www.cpsc.gov/FAQ/Component-Part-Testing); [16 CFR 1109.5 (Cornell LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/1109.5)
- **OEKO-TEX modular principle**: STANDARD 100 and LEATHER STANDARD explicitly allow certified source materials. Yarn, fabric and trims certified at any stage are recognized downstream, which "avoids double testing and lowers certification costs". Finished certificates state "produced using components pre-certified" (fully or partly). ECO PASSPORT-certified chemicals further reduce testing. — [Hohenstein modular system](https://www.hohenstein.de/en-us/oeko-tex/modular-system)
- Reuse caveats in the OEKO-TEX practice: the pre-certified supplier must match the user's product class and annex. Scope and validity must be checked regularly. Because limit values update yearly, **pre-certified stock stored ≥1 year may need spot checks**. — [TESTEX tips](https://www.testex.com/en/news/oeko-tex-standard-100-and-eco-passport-synergies-tips-and-tricks)
- Real-world failure mode: in 2023 **YKK alerted Fjällräven and other brands that its paint suppliers had added PFAS to the enamel on some hard components**. A PFAS-free water-repellent zipper tape therefore does not prove the slider or hardware is PFAS-free. — [GearJunkie](https://gearjunkie.com/news/pfas-outdoor-gear-pfc-ykk-zippers)
- Supplier "PFAS-free" claims are usually *intentionally-added* claims, not absence claims. GORE-TEX ePE is footnoted "Made without intentionally added PFAS; may contain trace amounts". — [Shimano GORE-TEX page](https://bike.shimano.com/en-NA/technologies/details/gore-tex.html)

### Inferences
- **Two evidence tiers fit the law.** (a) A signed supplier *intentionally-added (IA) declaration* is the operative proof for IA-standard states. (b) A **TOF/TF test report per homogeneous material** at an accredited lab is needed for CA/VT (TOF ≤50 ppm from 2027) and to rebut or avoid Washington's >50 ppm TF presumption. A single TOF result of ≤50 ppm clears all numeric thresholds found (CA 2027, VT 2027, WA presumption). So "≤50 ppm TOF, per component, with LOD ≤10 ppm" is a sensible network-wide pass bar for 2027.
- Component testing is not only *legitimate* but more conservative for CA if the threshold applies to "product components". Testing the assembled garment as one composite could dilute a fluorinated trim below 50 ppm and give a false pass. The rules engine should therefore require **every component** of a SKU's bill of materials to carry a passing attestation (fabric, membrane, DWR-treated shell, zipper tape, slider coating, thread, label, packaging).
- Certificate validity model (borrowing CPSC 1109 and OEKO-TEX practice): scope each certificate to {supplier, material ID, formulation/finish version, production lot or date range}. Give it an expiry (e.g., 12 months, matching OEKO-TEX annual updates), and invalidate it on any finish or chemical-supplier change. The YKK enamel case shows the risk comes from **tier-2 chemical suppliers changing inputs**.
- Unit economics of reuse: if one €300 TOF test on a mill's fabric is reused by 20 brands, the cost per brand falls to ~€15. A lab's volume tiers (Scion: NZ$1,100 → NZ$295) also reward the network for batching samples.
- Because CIC can't distinguish PFAS from other organofluorines (e.g., fluorinated dyes or pharmaceutical residues), the platform needs a "TOF positive, targeted negative, explanation" workflow, i.e., WA's rebuttal path.

### Gaps
- No US list prices or turnaround SLAs were found for TOF/CIC on textiles from SGS, Intertek, Eurofins, Bureau Veritas or Pace. The SGS TIC Mall page shows a price range but could not be fetched. **Get written quotes for: TOF screen, TOF + EN 17681-1 targeted, and rush fees.**
- I could not confirm whether AB 1817 names a specific TOF test method (my unverified recollection is that it does not), or whether DTSC/CalRecycle has issued method guidance.
- No published lab policy was found on "certificate reuse by third parties" (e.g., letter-of-authorization or report-sharing terms at SGS/Intertek). Labs typically issue reports to the paying client, and redistribution rights need contracting. This is unverified and should be asked of labs directly.
- EOF (extractable organic fluorine) method specifics and costs for textiles were not found.
- Cookware coatings (PTFE/sol-gel) and cosmetics testing approaches were not researched. TOF on a PTFE-coated pan is trivially positive; cookware is IA-declaration driven.

## Q2. Data standards: supplier-declaration formats, material identity, DPP models

### Takeaway
Supplier-declaration formats already exist and carry substance-level data: IPC-1752A (electronics, with CDX/IMDS ecosystems), the IEC 62474 DSL (which lists PFCA groups) and full material declarations. None is natural for apparel trims or textiles. The textile world runs on **certificate ecosystems**: OEKO-TEX (modular, reusable), ZDHC Gateway (chemical-level, levels 0–3) and AFIRM/AAFA RSLs. The best architectural precedents for "test once, reuse across brands" are ZDHC Gateway (a shared, verified registry) and Assent's network, where a supplier answers once and shares with many customers. The EU DPP (ESPR) gives a 2027–28 schema and identifier tailwind: GS1 Digital Link is one permitted implementation, not mandated.

### Inferences
- **Do not invent a new declaration format.** Model the attestation as a small JSON object (a "PFAS Attestation Record") that can export to IPC-1752A Class C/D style XML for hardlines/electronics brands, and into the fields a DPP will likely require. Core fields: material ID, supplier legal entity, IA declaration (Y/N, signer, date), TOF result + LOD + method (EN 14582/CIC), lab, accreditation, report hash, lot/date-range scope, expiry, and a list of BOM components covered.
- **Identity layer**: mills and trim suppliers rarely have GTINs for materials, and GTINs identify trade items, not fabric lots. Use a platform material ID (supplier + article number + finish version), optionally mapped to a GS1 GTIN or GS1 Digital Link URI when one exists. Brand SKUs link to materials via a BOM table.
- The ZDHC Gateway model (verification levels 0–3, visibility only after verification) is directly reusable as a **trust-tier UI**: L0 supplier self-declaration → L1 lab TOF report → L2 lab report + lot/chain-of-custody → L3 independent sampling (lab pulls the sample from the mill). This answers "how much should a brand trust a reused certificate?"
- Assent's 2% PFAS-positive rate implies the overwhelming majority of declarations are negative or absence statements. A proof-of-absence network's asset is exactly this negative data, made *verifiable* (test-backed, lot-scoped) instead of a questionnaire.
- Incumbents (Assent, Source Intelligence, osapiens, Certivo) are survey/BOM platforms aimed at manufacturers' compliance teams, not storefront enforcement. None was found that ties attestations to checkout blocking, which is the differentiation.

### Gaps
- AAFA RSL / AFIRM RSL PFAS entries and limits were not retrieved (search budget exhausted). Check the current AAFA RSL (edition 24/25) and the AFIRM RSL directly.
- No current counts for ZDHC Gateway products or brands were found.
- IMDS (automotive) PFAS handling, and whether IPC-1752A has a PFAS-specific declaration class, were not confirmed.
- Whether bluesign's FINDER or system substances list includes PFAS bans was not researched (bluesign is a likely competitor or partner).
- Final ESPR textile DPP data fields are not yet published (as of 2026-10-09).

## Q3. Legal: "intentionally added", accepted proof by state, liability for reused certificates

### Takeaway
"Intentionally added" (IA) states (Maine, Minnesota, New York, Connecticut, New Mexico, Rhode Island in part) generally require no routine testing. The documentation is a **manufacturer certificate of compliance (CoC) produced on agency request**, typically within 30 days in Maine, backed by "test results or other evidence" in Minnesota. California requires a written CoC from manufacturer to retailer and gives retailers **good-faith reliance** protection. Washington makes a TF test result (>50 ppm) legally meaningful as a presumption. Litigation risk is concentrated on **marketing claims** ("PFAS-free") rather than state bans, and courts have dismissed suits where plaintiffs didn't test the specific product. That puts a premium on lot-specific test evidence. Liability for a wrong reused certificate stays with the brand/manufacturer as certifier. The platform should be a records/evidence provider with contractual disclaimers and lab-held liability for analytical errors.

### Inferences
- **Does IA mean attestation-only is acceptable?** For ME, MN, NY, CT, NM and IL (2032), and for RI's IA-based categories, the law's trigger is intent, so a **supplier/manufacturer IA declaration is the primary proof**. Agencies can demand a CoC "with test results or other supporting evidence" (MN) on request (ME: 30 days). For CA/VT (numeric TOF) and WA (TF presumption), a test result becomes the de facto proof. MVP: store IA declarations for all states, and require TOF tests only for SKUs sold into CA/VT/WA or carrying "PFAS-free" marketing.
- The litigation pattern rewards **lot-specific test evidence for the exact product/model**. REI won because the plaintiff hadn't tested the model, and the inverse holds: brands defending claims want their own model-level evidence. A network linking SKU → BOM → component lot → test report is a litigation-defense asset as well as a compliance one.
- **Liability allocation for reused certificates** (recommended contract stack):
  - Lab: liable for analytical accuracy per its terms.
  - Supplier: warrants the IA declaration and that the tested lot/finish represents shipped goods, and indemnifies brands for false declarations.
  - Brand: remains the legal certifier (CA CoC signer, ME/MN manufacturer), uses the network as due-care evidence.
  - Platform: a records system that warrants only data integrity (hash-matching report to lab, timestamping, version scoping), not chemical truth, with E&O insurance.
  - Avoid any platform-issued "PFAS-free certified" mark; even OEKO-TEX refuses to certify "PFAS-free".
- **Rules engine complexity is real but bounded.** It needs about 12 states × about 15 categories × effective dates × exemption flags (severe-wet outdoor apparel, PPE, internal/electronic components, used goods, manufacture-date exemptions like WA's) × evidence standards (IA vs TOF ppm vs TF presumption) × labeling-instead-of-ban paths (CT 2026, RI 2029, CA severe-wet disclosure). Some outcomes are "label required" rather than "block", so the engine needs three outputs: **allow / allow-with-disclosure / block**.
- Near-term trigger dates founders can sell against: **2027-01-01** (CA 50 ppm, VT 50 ppm, WA apparel ban, RI cookware/textiles, NM cookware/juvenile/labeling, WA reporting 2027-01-31) and **2028-01-01** (CA/NY severe-wet outdoor exemption sunset, CT ban, NM wave 2).

### Gaps
- Rhode Island, Colorado, Vermont and Connecticut **documentation/proof requirements** (CoC on request? testing?) were not found. Verify RI DEM, CO CDPHE, VT ANR and CT DEEP guidance.
- AB 1817 enforcement agency and the exact good-faith reliance text were not verified (secondary sources conflict on the "knew or should have known" caveat).
- A Hunton (Feb 2026) count of "18 states with PFAS product restrictions" surfaced in search, but its URL was not captured, so it is unverified.

- Whether federal TSCA §8(a)(7) PFAS reporting (EPA) interacts with this was not researched.

## Q4. Geo-fenced commerce: how platforms let merchants block SKU × state, existing tools, build effort

### Takeaway
On Shopify, the correct primitive is a **Cart and Checkout Validation Function** (server-side, can't be bypassed). Public App Store apps using Functions work on **all plans (Basic+)**, while custom apps with Functions need Plus. Shopify's own docs list "preventing shipping to restricted locations" as a use case. Existing state-restriction apps are cheap ($9.99–$49.99/month) and rule-based (the merchant types rules). None found is driven by a maintained regulatory dataset or tied to compliance evidence, which is the wedge. ShipperHQ covers the same need for Magento/BigCommerce. A Shopify MVP (Function + admin app + metafield sync) is a modest build.

### Cited Findings
- Shopify recommends server-side validation via Shopify Functions so that "a merchant's business logic is enforced". Client-side validation can be bypassed. Validation runs for online store carts, custom-storefront carts and checkout. — [Shopify.dev: cart & checkout validation](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/build/checkout/cart-checkout-validation); [Shopify.dev validation overview](https://shopify.dev/apps/checkout/validation)
- Checkout UI extensions rendering on the information, shipping and payment steps are **Shopify Plus only**. — [Shopify.dev](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/build/checkout/cart-checkout-validation)
- Shopify Help: **public apps built with Shopify Functions are available on Basic and higher; custom apps built with Functions are Plus-only**. — [Shopify Help Center: checkout customization](https://help.shopify.com/manual/checkout-settings/checkout-customization); third-party confirmation from [Shero Commerce](https://sherocommerce.com/blogs/insights/how-to-customize-the-shopify-checkout-without-plus)

- One source claims Shopify Scripts stopped running on 2026-06-30, pushing all checkout logic to Functions (**unverified**). — surfaced via [Identixweb](https://www.identixweb.com/shopify-functions/)

  - **Cart Block** (checkout validator by quantity, country, customer, discount code). — [Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/cart-block). **DS: Address Validation/Block**. — [Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/validify)

### Inferences
- **Build effort (estimate, not sourced):** a Shopify public app with (1) a Cart/Checkout Validation Function reading `deliveryAddress.provinceCode` and a per-variant metafield of blocked states, (2) an embedded admin app to map SKUs → BOM → attestations and compute the blocked-state list per SKU, and (3) a nightly rules-engine sync writing metafields. That is roughly **6–10 engineer-weeks** for a production MVP, including App Store review. The hard part is the regulatory dataset and evidence model, not the checkout code.
- Additional enforcement points the MVP should cover, because validation only guards checkout:
  - **order-level holds** for draft orders, POS, B2B, and orders imported from marketplaces or wholesale (via order webhooks + fulfillment holds)
  - storefront messaging ("not available in your state", like Compliant Commerce's SEO-preserving approach)
  - an **audit log of blocked checkouts** as compliance evidence (ZIP Lock already logs blocks)
  - labeling outputs for "allow-with-disclosure" states (CT 2026, CA severe-wet outdoor apparel, NM labeling 2027), e.g., injecting a "Made with PFAS chemicals" notice on PDPs for online sales
- **Price anchor**: generic geo-restriction apps cost $10–$50/mo with merchant-typed rules. A PFAS app with a *maintained regulatory dataset + evidence vault* can plausibly charge $99–$499/mo by SKU count. This is an inference by analogy to Compliant Commerce's $49.99 "regulated vertical" premium; no PFAS-specific comparable was found.
- The geo-fencing half is most valuable to merchants who **still sell PFAS-containing SKUs nationally**: cookware with PTFE coatings (banned in MN 2025, ME/VT/CO 2026, NM/RI 2027, CT 2028, but legal in CA after the SB 682 veto and in most states), severe-wet outdoor apparel during exemption windows, ski wax, and fabric treatments. Apparel brands mostly reformulate, so for them the attestation half matters more.

### Gaps
- Shopify Functions execution limits (instruction count, input size, number of validation functions per store), whether validations run in accelerated checkouts (Shop Pay/Apple Pay express) and Buy with Prime, and how Markets interacts were not verified (docs unreachable).
- WooCommerce plugins (e.g., conditional shipping/payments) and Salesforce Commerce Cloud/commercetools mechanisms were not researched (search budget exhausted).
- Amazon/Walmart Marketplace state-level PFAS listing controls are unknown. Marketplaces don't expose per-state SKU blocking to sellers in the way Shopify does (unverified).
- Install counts and revenue for Ship Restrict, ZIP Lock and Compliant Commerce are unknown. Ship Restrict reportedly had no reviews at the time of search.

## Q5. Go-to-market: who first, channels, pricing benchmarks, cold-start

### Takeaway
Seed the **supply side with the handful of performance-material and trim suppliers who already market "PFAS-free"** to hundreds of brands: Gore ePE, Polartec, YKK DynaPel, plus Taiwan/Vietnam mills that supply outdoor brands. Sell first to **outdoor and performance apparel brands**, which face a hard cliff on 2027-01-01 (CA/VT 50 ppm TOF, WA ban) and 2028-01-01 (CA/NY severe-wet exemption sunset, CT ban). Then add **cookware/ski-wax/fabric-treatment DTC merchants** as the geo-fencing use case. Test labs are natural channel partners: they gain volume and the platform gains verified data. Precedents (OEKO-TEX modular certs, ZDHC Gateway, Assent's "answer once, share many") show the network model works but is crowded on surveys. ### Inferences
- **Who first (recommended sequence):**
  1. **Design partners (months 0–3):** 3–5 mid-size outdoor/performance DTC brands on Shopify ($10M–$100M revenue) with severe-wet or DWR products and CA/NY/WA exposure. They feel the 2027-01-01 and 2028-01-01 cliffs, lack Assent-scale budgets, and buy from the same supplier set.
  2. **Seed supply (in parallel):** ask each design partner for its top 20 materials/trims, then recruit those suppliers with a free listing, a lab-batched TOF test at network-subsidized cost, and the pitch "one upload ends 50 brand questionnaires." Priority suppliers: membrane/laminate makers (Gore ePE, Polartec), zipper/trim suppliers (YKK, plus the hardware-coating risk shown in 2023), DWR chemistry makers (whose ZDHC Gateway/ECO PASSPORT status can be imported), and the Taiwan/Vietnam mills behind outdoor brands.
  3. **Second vertical: cookware, ski wax and fabric-treatment DTC merchants**, for the geo-fencing app. Their PTFE/PFAS SKUs are legal in many states but banned in MN, ME, VT, CO, NM, RI and CT on staggered dates, so state-level blocking is a revenue-saving feature, not just risk avoidance.
  4. **Retailers later:** CA's CoC good-faith reliance and Maine's "retailers liable only after notice" mean retailers mainly need to *collect* CoCs from vendors, which makes them a natural distribution channel (vendor-onboarding mandates) once the network has density.
- **Cold start**: seed with categories where (a) a few suppliers sit in many brands' BOMs (zippers, membranes, DWR-treated shell fabrics, sewing thread, labels/packaging) and (b) regulation forces a test (CA/VT TOF, WA TF). Import existing evidence where suppliers allow it (OEKO-TEX certificates, ZDHC Gateway levels, supplier IA declarations) as Level 0/1 trust tiers, so the network shows non-zero coverage on day one.
- **Labs as channel**: offer SGS, Intertek, BV, Eurofins or a nimble independent lab a "report-to-network" API. Each report issued with client consent is pushed as a verified, hash-matched attestation, giving the lab volume and the platform verified data. Revenue could come from a referral or batching fee. The volume discounts above make network-batched sampling (e.g., quarterly "PFAS test days" per supplier) attractive.
- **Events/associations (not verified in this session; see Gaps)**: Outdoor Retailer, Performance Days and Functional Fabric Fair for outdoor material suppliers; AFIRM/AAFA for RSL alignment; Cookware Sustainability Alliance for cookware (likely adversarial to bans but needs geo-compliance); Textile Exchange and Cascale for sustainability teams.
- **Pricing hypothesis:**
  - Suppliers: free listing, paid "verified" tier ($1–5k/yr) plus pass-through lab costs.
  - Brands: $300–$1,500/mo by SKU/BOM count for the attestation vault, CoC generation (CA, ME on request, MN), and state reports.
  - Shopify geo-fence app: $49–$299/mo, standalone or bundled.
  - Enterprise/retailer: annual contracts.
  - These are anchored to Shopify vertical-compliance apps ($10–$50/mo) at the bottom. Enterprise PFAS survey platforms sit at the top; their pricing was not found.
