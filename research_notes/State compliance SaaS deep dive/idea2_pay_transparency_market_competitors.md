## Q1. Which US states and cities have pay-transparency job-posting laws in effect as of Oct 2026, and what are their key terms (remote coverage, private right of action, damages, cure)?

### Takeaway
As of 2026-10-09, about **14 states plus DC require a pay range in the posting itself**: CA, CO, CT (from Oct 1, 2026), HI, IL, ME, MD, MA, MN, NJ, NY, VT, VA, WA, plus DC. Delaware's law starts Sept 26, 2027, and Columbus OH starts enforcing Jan 1, 2027. The claim "16 states plus DC as of Aug 1, 2026" is **accurate only if it counts all statewide pay-disclosure laws**, including the on-request laws in NV and RI and CT's pre-October on-request law. It is **not** a count of posting mandates, which stood at about 13 states plus DC on Aug 1, 2026. Only **WA, VA (new July 2026) and CT (new Oct 2026)** give applicants a clear private right of action with money damages, and that is where the litigation risk sits.

**Washington (RCW 49.58.110): the litigation engine**
- Covers employers with 15+ employees. Postings must include a wage scale or salary range and a general description of benefits and other compensation. Recruiting for any position that "could be performed remotely by a Washington resident" can make an employer a target. — [Buchalter via Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=35cf877a-dec1-4498-ad1e-f40170d723ca)
- Private right of action for applicants and employees. Before July 27, 2025, damages were statutory damages of $5,000 per violation (or actual damages if greater) plus fees. — [Fox Rothschild](https://www.foxrothschild.com/publications/washington-state-may-ease-pay-transparency-rules); [Clockspot](https://www.clockspot.com/articles/pay-transparency-laws-by-state)
- **SSB 5408 (2025)**: signed May 20, 2025 and effective **July 27, 2025** (some sources say June 27; most say July 27). It is not retroactive. Its main changes are below. — [Perkins Coie](https://perkinscoie.com/insights/update/washington-state-amends-equal-pay-law-clarify-employer-requirements-and-liability); [Mondaq](https://mondaq.com/unitedstates/employee-benefits-compensation/1616400/washington-state-makes-key-changes-to-amend-equal-pay-and-opportunities-act); [Trusaic](https://trusaic.com/blog/washington-state-updates-pay-transparency-law-with-sb-5408/)
  - **Cure period:** a written notice from any person starts a **5-business-day** window. Within it, the employer must correct the posting **and notify any third-party posting entity to correct it**. If the employer cures in time, no damages or penalties can be assessed by a court or by L&I. The cure applies to postings made **on or before July 27, 2027**, so it **sunsets**. — [O'Hagan Meyer](https://ohaganmeyer.com/2025/06/19/washington-amends-equal-pay-and-opportunities-act); [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2025/05/washington-state-clarifies-its-pay-transparency-law)
  - **Damages:** the flat $5,000 became a **sliding scale of $100 to $5,000 per violation**, with discretion based on factors such as willfulness. Commentators expect litigation over whether the scale applies to pending cases. — [Epstein Becker Green workforcebulletin](https://www.workforcebulletin.com/pay-transparency-developments-washington-limits-its-equal-pay-opportunities-act-in-2025-amendments); [Beresford Law](https://beresfordlaw.com/?p=17061)
  - **Scraped-posting carve-out:** the definition of "posting" now excludes a "solicitation for recruiting job applicants that is digitally replicated and published without an employer's consent." — [Trusaic](https://trusaic.com/blog/washington-state-updates-pay-transparency-law-with-sb-5408/); [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2025/05/washington-state-clarifies-its-pay-transparency-law)
  - Employers may now post a single fixed pay amount when only one rate is offered. — [Fox Rothschild](https://www.foxrothschild.com/publications/washington-state-may-ease-pay-transparency-rules)
- No 2026-session amendments to the EPOA turned up in searches. — (search across [Perkins Coie](https://perkinscoie.com/insights/update/washington-state-amends-equal-pay-law-clarify-employer-requirements-and-liability), [Littler](https://www.littler.com/news-analysis/asap/relief-steep-pay-transparency-penalties-businesses-washington-state), [IADC](https://www.iadclaw.org/defensecounseljournal/washington-state-pay-transparency-class-actions-a-wild-ride/)). **[Absence of evidence, not confirmed]**

**Virginia (SB 215 / HB 636), effective July 1, 2026: a new private-action state**
- Covers all employers with no size threshold. Every public or internal posting needs a good-faith wage or salary range, and the breadth of the range is a factor in judging good faith. The law also bans asking for salary history. — [DLA Piper Knowledge](https://knowledge.dlapiper.com/dlapiperknowledge/globalemploymentlatestdevelopments/2026/virginia-pay-transparency-law-goes-into-effect-july-1-2026); [Morgan Lewis (May 2026)](https://www.morganlewis.com/ru/pubs/2026/05/virginia-law-will-require-pay-transparency-restrict-employers-from-seeking-wage-history)
- **Private right of action**: an aggrieved applicant or employee can sue within 1 year for **actual damages** plus legal and equitable relief. Civil penalties run up to $1,000 for a first violation and up to $5,000 for later ones. An earlier pre-signature summary describing "$10,000 statutory damages" does **not** match the enacted text quoted later. — [JD Supra](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/virginia-enacts-pay-transparency-and-2633025/); [Brightmine](https://hrcenter.us.brightmine.com/news/virginia-pay-transparency-law-awaits-signature/54799)
- **Cure**: after written notice from anyone, the employer has **15 business days** to correct the posting "on the original posting locations." If it does, no action may be brought. — [Epstein Becker Green](https://epstein.sitepilot11.firmseek.com/workforce-bulletin/virginia-pay-transparency-requirements-take-effect-july-1-2026)
- **Remote coverage is unclear in the statute.** Practitioners advise treating remote roles that can be performed from VA as covered, and stating which states a remote role excludes. — [Foley (May 2026)](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2026/05/trends-of-pay-transparency-laws-and-salary-history-bans-continue-to-grow-new-laws-enacted-in-virginia-maine-and-delaware/); [SixFifty](https://www.sixfifty.com/blog/virginia-pay-transparency-law-requirements/)

**Connecticut (Public Act 26-12 / HB 5003), posting mandate effective Oct 1, 2026**
- Covers all employers regardless of size. Internal and external postings, including intranet and internal-email job notices, must carry the wage range and a general description of benefits. "Wage range" now means a range set in good faith. — [Pullman & Comley](https://www.pullcom.com/working-together/2026-session-connecticut-general-assembly-labor-and-employment-laws-public-act-26-12); [Foley (Sept 2026)](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2026/09/connecticut-expands-pay-transparency-requirements-starting-october-1-2026/); [Seyfarth](https://www.seyfarth.com/news-insights/connecticut-expands-pay-transparency-requirements-under-hb-5003.html)
- **Private cause of action** for applicants and employees: compensatory damages, attorneys' fees, and legal or equitable relief. Punitive damages are eliminated. The statute of limitations is 2 years. — [Littler](https://www.littler.com/news-analysis/asap/public-act-no-26-12-gamechanger-connecticut-workplace-compliance-here-are); [Vorys](https://www.vorys.com/publication-connecticut-employers-prepare-for-enhanced-pay-transparency-requirements)
- **Remote:** an out-of-state remote role is covered only if it reports to a CT-based supervisor or worksite. — [Polsinelli](https://www.polsinelli.com/public-accommodations-accessibility/polsinelli-at-work-blog/connecticut-wage-transparency-pay-codes-job-posting-compliance)
- No cure period was found in the sources. **[Gap]**

**California (Lab. Code § 432.3 / SB 1162, since Jan 1, 2023)**
- Covers employers with 15+ employees. The range must be what the employer "reasonably expects to pay." A link or QR code is not enough. **If the employer uses a third party to publish a posting, the employer must give the pay scale to that third party, and the third party must include it.** — [DLSE instructions](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/Pay-Trans-Instructions.pdf); [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/hunton-employment-labor-perspectives/california-pay-transparency-law-recent-dlse-guidance)
- Enforcement is through a Labor Commissioner complaint (within 1 year) or a civil action for injunctive relief. Penalties are **$100 to $10,000 per violation**. A first violation reportedly carries no penalty if the employer updates its postings. — [DLSE](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/Pay-Trans-Instructions.pdf); [SPZ Legal](https://spzlegal.com/employment/new-california-salary-transparency-law) **[cure detail UNVERIFIED]**
- SB 642 (Stats. 2025, Ch. 468), effective Jan 1, 2026, extends the Equal Pay Act statute of limitations to 3 years. — [Clockspot](https://www.clockspot.com/research/pay-transparency-laws-by-state) **[UNVERIFIED]**

**Colorado (EPEWA Part 2; CDLE INFO #9A, May 29, 2024)**
- All employers must disclose pay and other information in internal and external postings. For remote roles that could be done in Colorado, the posting must state the Colorado pay. — [CDLE INFO #9A](https://cdle.colorado.gov/sites/cdle/files/info_%239a_transparency_in_pay_and_job_opportunities_the_colorado_epewa_part_2_05.29.24.pdf); [Jackson Lewis](https://www.jacksonlewis.com/insights/colorado-pay-transparency-more-guidance-job-promotional-posting-requirements-issued)
- No private right of action for posting violations, per ADP and a vendor summary. — [ADP](https://www.adp.com/spark/articles/2023/04/4-potential-consequences-of-violating-pay-transparency-laws.aspx) **[penalty amounts not confirmed in this session]**

**Illinois (820 ILCS 112/10(b-25), since Jan 1, 2025)**
- Covers employers with 15+ employees and positions performed at least partly in Illinois. There is **no private cause of action**; the Illinois Department of Labor (IDOL) enforces, and complaints must be filed within 1 year. Penalties for an active posting: 1st offense, 14-day cure and up to $500; 2nd, 7-day cure and up to $2,500; 3rd and later, no cure and up to $10,000. Inactive postings: up to $250, $2,500 and $10,000. Records must be kept for 5 years. — [IDOL](https://labor.illinois.gov/pay); [Perkins Coie](https://perkinscoie.com/insights/update/illinois-pay-transparency-requirements-arrive); [SHRM](https://shrm.org/topics-tools/employment-law-compliance/10-key-takeaways-from-new-illinois-pay-transparency-job)

**New York State (Labor Law § 194-b) and NYC**
- NYS: no private right of action. Penalties are $1,000, $2,000, then $3,000 per violation. Covers jobs performed at least partly in NY, including out-of-state roles reporting to a NY supervisor or office. **NYC: civil penalties up to $250,000** (NYC Admin Code § 8-107(32)). — [ADP](https://www.adp.com/spark/articles/2023/04/4-potential-consequences-of-violating-pay-transparency-laws.aspx); [Fisher Phillips](https://www.fisherphillips.com/en/news-insights/new-york-proposes-pay-transparency-regulations.html); [Brightmine](https://www.brightmine.com/us/resources/u-s-pay-transparency-laws-by-state-and-locality/)

**New Jersey, Massachusetts, Vermont, Maine, Delaware, and others**
- **NJ** (effective June 1, 2025): 10+ employees. Postings need the wage range and a benefits description. Penalties are assessed **per position, not per posting**; one source says up to $600 per violation. NJDOL has started active enforcement. — [Ogletree](https://ogletree.com/insights-resources/blog-posts/pay-transparency-reminder-5-states-laws-take-effect-in-2025); [DirectEmployers](https://directemployers.org/2025/05/05/more-states-enacting-pay-transparency-laws-what-employers-in-new-jersey-vermont-need-to-know/)

- **VT** (effective July 1, 2025): 5+ employees. Covers remote roles that "predominantly perform work for" a Vermont location. Enforced by the Attorney General; no private action. — [Epstein Becker Green](https://www.workforcebulletin.com/coast-to-coast-pay-transparency-continues-to-trend-in-2025)
- **ME** (effective July 29, 2026, though one firm says June 29): 10+ employees. Covers internal postings and **postings placed through a third party**. Silent on remote roles. No private right of action; Maine DOL fines of $100 to $500 per violation. — [Vorys](https://www.vorys.com/publication-maine-employers-prepare-for-new-pay-transparency-requirements); [JD Supra](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/maine-employers-prepare-for-new-pay-7345742/)

**Federal**
- No federal posting mandate exists. ### Inferences
- The counts reconcile as follows. The 16 states with statewide disclosure laws in effect on Aug 1, 2026 plus DC make 17 jurisdictions; adding enacted-but-not-effective Delaware gives the "18 jurisdictions" figure. Posting mandates were 13 states plus DC on Aug 1 and 14 states plus DC (15 jurisdictions) once CT took effect on Oct 1. Product messaging should say "14 states + DC + several cities require ranges in postings (Oct 2026)", not "16 states."
- The private-damages map is narrow but growing. **WA** (sliding $100 to $5,000, cure until mid-2027), **VA** (actual damages, 15-business-day cure, effective July 2026) and **CT** (compensatory damages plus fees, no cure found, effective Oct 2026) are the only states where applicants can plausibly bring money-damages class actions. NYC's private right reportedly covers current employees only (prior knowledge, not confirmed this session). Everywhere else, enforcement is administrative with modest fines and often a cure step.
- Remote-role rules vary a lot: CO (any role performable in CO), NY/CT/IL (reporting-line tests), VT (predominant-work test), VA/ME (silent), DE (DE-based employer's remote roles). That makes per-jurisdiction scoring of a posting a real logic problem, which favors a rules engine. It is also hard to keep current, as tracker disagreements show.

### Gaps
- Primary statute texts could not be opened. Exact damages formulas for HI, MD, MN, DC and MA, and whether MN/DC/HI/MD allow private suits, were **not confirmed** this session.
- No source confirmed NYC's private-right scope or its 30-day first-violation cure. That comes from prior knowledge and is **unverified**.
- Westchester County: no source found this session.
- Whether NY DOL finalized its 2023 proposed regulations (including the scraping carve-out) was not confirmed.

## Q2. Litigation: the Washington EPOA class-action wave and other states. Are employers sued over third-party or aggregator copies, and what do statutes and agencies say about third-party postings?

### Takeaway
The "~250 employers sued / ~$0.5B exposure" claim comes from the **Association of Washington Business**, an employer advocacy group, and describes **mid-2024** potential liability, not dollars paid. Later practitioner and vendor counts say **"well over 200"** to **"300+"** WA class actions, almost all in King County. **Settlements kept arriving through 2026**, typically **$0.4M to $4.75M per case**, mostly for postings made before the July 2025 amendment. Third-party copies were a real early driver: plaintiffs applied through Indeed, and AWB complained employers were sued over scraped postings. But by Oct 2026, **WA, CO, NY (proposed regulations) and IL all exclude scraped or unauthorized copies from employer liability**, while holding employers responsible for postings they **engaged** a third party to publish. That changes what a "scan every copy" product is actually for.

***Branson v. Washington Fine Wine & Spirits* (Wash. Sup. Ct., Sept 4, 2025, 6-3)**
- An applicant does not have to be "bona fide" to recover. Anyone who applies to a noncompliant posting qualifies, whatever their intent. The court did not decide other questions, such as whether applicants have a private right of action at all. In *Branson*, plaintiffs applied **through Indeed.com** postings that lacked pay information. — [Dorsey](https://www.dorsey.com/newsresources/publications/client-alerts/2025/9/washington-epoa); [K&L Gates](https://www.klgates.com/Washington-Supreme-Court-Adopts-Expansive-Definition-of-Job-Applicant-Under-EPOA-Pay-Transparency-Statute-9-8-2025); [Duane Morris](https://blogs.duanemorris.com/classactiondefense/2025/09/08/washington-supreme-court-rules-that-job-applicants-need-not-be-bona-fide-under-the-epoa-to-launch-class-actions/); [Hunton/Law360](https://www.hunton.com/insights/publications/wash-ruling-raises-pay-transparency-litigation-risk)
- Lockton's headline says the ruling "could lead to more litigation and EPL insurance claims." — [Lockton](https://global.lockton.com/us/en/news-insights/washington-supreme-court-ruling-could-lead-more-epl-insurance-claims)

**Third-party and aggregator postings: what each law says**
- **WA (before July 27, 2025):** employers were treated as liable for postings "maintained by the employer... or by third parties, such as Indeed.com." AWB said employers were sued "for a single deficient job posting, even in which the posted information was outside of the employer's control," because "job boards often scrape job listings and repost them without full wage data." — [search synthesis of AWB / Trusaic / practitioner notes](https://www.awb.org/was-pay-transparency-law-undergoes-key-fixes-but-challenges-remain/)
- **WA (after July 27, 2025):** postings "digitally replicated and published without an employer's consent" are excluded. The cure requires notifying third-party posting entities within 5 business days. Commentators say this closes the loophole that produced class actions over Indeed and LinkedIn copies. **No reported ruling has yet applied the carve-out.** — [Epstein Becker Green](https://www.workforcebulletin.com/pay-transparency-developments-washington-limits-its-equal-pay-opportunities-act-in-2025-amendments); [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2025/05/washington-state-clarifies-its-pay-transparency-law)
- **WA L&I** (2022 draft policy quoted by SHRM): "it is the employer's responsibility to assure continuous compliance... regardless of any use of third-party administrators." — [SHRM](https://shrm.org/topics-tools/employment-law-compliance/washington-updates-guidance-pay-transparency-rules) **[current final text not verified]**
- **CO (INFO #9A):** employers using LinkedIn or Indeed must submit a compliant posting. Third parties are not liable because they are not "employers." In the guidance's "Enrico's / LinkedDeed" example, **the employer is not in violation when a third party reproduces its compliant post without pay details.** — [CDLE INFO #9A](https://cdle.colorado.gov/sites/cdle/files/info_%239a_transparency_in_pay_and_job_opportunities_the_colorado_epewa_part_2_05.29.24.pdf); [CBA copy of INFO #9](https://Www.Cobar.org/Portals/COBAR/Repository/CBA/10322/CDLE%20INFO9_%20Equal%20Pay%20Transparency%20Rules%20(2021).pdf)
- **IL (IDOL):** a third party the employer **engaged** is covered and can be liable unless it shows the employer failed to supply pay information. Out-of-state or small size is no defense. "Job boards that 'scrape' postings from employer websites" without being engaged are **not covered**. — [IDOL pay page](https://labor.illinois.gov/pay); [IDOL FAQ](https://labor.illinois.gov/faqs/equal-pay-act-salary-transparency-faq.html); [Jackson Lewis](https://jacksonlewis.com/node/30581)
- **NY (proposed NYS DOL regulations, Sept 2023):** ads count whether posted directly or through a recruiter or job board, and the onus is on the employer. Employers are **not responsible for ads "scraped" or automatically aggregated without their knowledge or consent.** Final adoption was not confirmed. — [Jackson Lewis](https://www.jacksonlewis.com/insights/new-york-issues-guidance-proposes-regulations-newly-effective-pay-transparency-requirements); [Goldberg Segalla](https://www.goldbergsegalla.com/news-and-knowledge/knowledge/new-york-state-department-of-labor-releases-proposed-pay-transparency-regulations/)
- **CA:** an employer using a third party must provide the pay scale, and the third party must include it. — [DLSE](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/Pay-Trans-Instructions.pdf)
- **ME:** the law covers postings made through a third party. — [Vorys](https://www.vorys.com/publication-maine-employers-prepare-for-new-pay-transparency-requirements)
- **VA:** the cure means correcting "on the original posting locations." — [Epstein Becker Green](https://epstein.sitepilot11.firmseek.com/workforce-bulletin/virginia-pay-transparency-requirements-take-effect-july-1-2026)
- A contrary vendor claim says "in most states the requirement applies to the posting wherever it appears, including third-party copies." — [Joveo 2026 checklist](https://www.joveo.com/blog/job-posting-compliance-checklist/). This **conflicts** with the CO, IL, NY and WA guidance above.

**Other states' litigation**
- **California:** searches found **no** class actions or PAGA suits based on § 432.3 posting violations. — (search synthesis; [Cal. Lawyers Assn. litigation article](https://calawyers.org/publications/litigation/cal-litig-2023-volume-35-issue-3-californias-commitment-to-wage-transparency-comes-at-a-cost-to-employers/)) **[Gap, not proof of absence]**
- **CO, NY and IL** have no applicant private right of action for posting violations ([ADP](https://www.adp.com/spark/articles/2023/04/4-potential-consequences-of-violating-pay-transparency-laws.aspx); [IDOL](https://labor.illinois.gov/pay)), so no WA-style class waves were found there.
- **NJ:** reported fines range from $1,000 to $6,000 per violation, with total penalties over $500,000. These may reflect broader equal-pay enforcement. — [Payscale legislative lowdown](https://www.payscale.com/compensation-trends/the-legislative-lowdown-july-20-25) **[UNVERIFIED attribution]**

### Inferences
- The WA wave is now a **tail of legacy settlements**. Most 2026 settlements cover class periods that start in Jan 2023, i.e. postings made before the amendment. The cure window (through postings made by July 27, 2027), the $100 to $5,000 sliding scale and the scraping carve-out should sharply cut **new** WA filings on post-July-2025 postings, though no count confirms this yet. The cure **sunsets in July 2027**, after which uncured violations are exposed again.
- **The main legal value of a third-party scanner is not "you're liable for every scraped copy."** In CO, IL, NY and WA, scraped copies are carved out. The value is: (1) **finding consented or engaged copies** (paid boards, programmatic distribution, staffing agencies, ATS syndication feeds) that drop or garble pay fields, since those *are* the employer's liability; (2) **evidence that a copy was unauthorized**, via an audit trail showing the source and no consent; (3) **meeting cure deadlines** (WA 5 business days including notifying third-party hosts; VA 15 business days on original locations; IL 14 and 7 days) with a timestamped log; (4) catching stale reposts of postings whose ranges are now inaccurate.
- New plaintiff fronts are **VA (since July 2026)** and **CT (since Oct 2026)**. CT has no cure period in the sources found, so it could be the next WA-style venue. VA's 15-day cure and actual-damages-only remedy make class economics weaker there.

### Gaps
- No docket-based count of WA filings in 2025–2026, and no data on filings after July 27, 2025.
- No court has yet construed WA's "without employer's consent" carve-out, for example for an employer-shared posting that a vendor re-syndicates.
- No VA or CT suits found yet. Both laws are too new.
- No data on defense costs per WA case.

## Q3. Market: how many employers and postings are in scope, what share of postings lacks pay ranges, how big is EPLI, and are insurers pricing or excluding pay-transparency risk?

### Takeaway
About **half of US Indeed postings still lack explicit pay** (49% included pay as of late June 2026, flat for about a year), so the noncompliance surface is large. Coverage is partly driven by remote roles, which can pull in employers nationwide. Credible counts of employers posting into covered jurisdictions were **not found**. EPLI coverage of pay-transparency suits is **contested**: carriers are split, some treat these as wage-and-hour claims (often defense-only sublimits), and no court has resolved it. That leaves employers with uninsured exposure, a buying motive. EPLI market-size figures are low-quality market-research estimates of **about $4–5B globally (2025)**.

### Cited Findings

North America is the largest region (about 36% in one report). **Low-quality, conflicting estimates.**

The market is "stable but firming," with higher claim frequency ([Aon 2025](https://aon.com/en/insights/articles/financial-services-group/management-liability-insurance-market-in-2025-stability-amid-evolving-risks)).

### Inferences
- With roughly half of postings lacking pay nationally, and remote postings potentially reaching CO, WA, NY, CT, VA and others, a large share of US employers with 15+ employees that post remote roles probably have some in-scope postings. Indeed's suppression policy in some jurisdictions means the remaining risk sits in **non-Indeed channels**: company career pages, niche boards, staffing-agency reposts and programmatic feeds.

It also suggests EPLI carriers may prefer exclusions or sublimits over buying scanning tools, though no evidence of carrier tool procurement was found.

### Inferences
- **Commoditization risk:** detecting a missing pay range on a posting is cheap (Apify at $5 per 1,000) and already bundled into job-description and ATS tools. - Most likely fast followers or acquirers: Payscale (which now owns Datapeople), Ongig, SixFifty, and programmatic vendors (Joveo, Appcast) that already control distribution feeds and could add "copy audit" cheaply.

- Not researched (search budget exhausted): **Pave, Pequity, Ravio, Figures, Salary.com, Mercer, Textio, Greenhouse, Workday, iCIMS native pay fields, Appcast, PandoLogic, LinkedIn job-policy specifics**, and law-firm tech offerings (e.g., Littler/Ogletree compliance products).
- Date and terms of the Payscale–Datapeople acquisition not confirmed.

## Q5. Business feasibility: willingness to pay, procurement by defense firms and EPLI carriers, pricing benchmarks, and risks

### Takeaway
WTP evidence is strongest for **mid-to-large employers with WA, CT or VA exposure**. WA settlements of **$0.4M–$4.75M** (2025–2026), contested EPLI coverage, and *Branson*'s broad standing rule make even a $20K–$50K/yr tool cheap insurance. But the **core thesis is weakened**: WA's 2025 amendment, CO and IL guidance, and NY's proposed rules **excuse scraped third-party copies**, and new WA filings should drop through mid-2027 while the cure applies. The product's strongest legal role is **cure-window execution, provenance evidence, and monitoring of consented distribution channels**, with notable upside if the WA cure sunsets in July 2027 and if CT litigation takes off. No evidence was found that defense firms or EPLI carriers procure scanning tools.

  - *Subsiding WA wave:* the 5-day cure, scraped-posting carve-out and $100–$5,000 scale apply to postings after July 27, 2025. AWB argues the suits reflected serial plaintiffs, not widespread noncompliance. — [AWB](https://www.awb.org/was-pay-transparency-law-undergoes-key-fixes-but-challenges-remain/); [Perkins Coie](https://perkinscoie.com/insights/update/washington-state-amends-equal-pay-law-clarify-employer-requirements-and-liability)
  - *Cure sunset:* WA cure applies only to postings through July 27, 2027. — [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2025/05/washington-state-clarifies-its-pay-transparency-law)
  - *Federal preemption:* low. ### Inferences
- **Best initial buyer:** multi-state employers (roughly 500+ employees, heavy remote and hourly hiring in retail, hospitality, logistics and tech) already sued or demand-lettered in WA, plus their **outside defense counsel**, who could use the tool to document provenance and cure, and to argue lack of consent under the WA carve-out. EPLI carriers look like a **channel or partner** (risk-control service bundled with policies) rather than direct buyers, given no procurement evidence.
