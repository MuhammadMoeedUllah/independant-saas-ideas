## Q1. US packaging EPR laws and status as of Oct 2026 (producer definitions, exemptions, deadlines, fee start dates, litigation)

### Takeaway
Seven states have packaging EPR laws: ME, OR, CO, CA, MN, MD and WA. No new state enacted one in the 2026 sessions found (NJ re-filed, NY did not pass, TN tabled). Only Oregon (since mid-2025) and Colorado (since January 2026) are invoicing regular program fees. California charged a small one-time "Early Fee" in August 2026, and its full program fees start in 2027 once CalRecycle approves CAA's plan. MD, MN and WA fees do not begin until 2028–2030, and Maine is stalled with no stewardship organization. Litigation is live but trending toward the states. Oregon's law was upheld in full after a bench trial (Aug 27–28, 2026) and is now on appeal to the 9th Circuit. NAW has also sued California and Colorado, and a separate state-court suit (ILMA) is pending in Colorado.

### Inferences
- Demand is real today in only two fee-paying states (OR and CO), plus California from 2027. California is the largest single driver, with a projected 2027 program budget of $1.26B–$1.87B plus the $500M/yr surcharge. MD, MN and WA add fee exposure only from 2028–2030, and Maine is stalled. A simulator launched in late 2026 sells mostly on "CA 2027 + OR + CO," with the other states as "coming" line items.
- The "converter as producer" angle is narrow. It applies to service/foodservice packaging in Oregon (first seller), to unbranded or private-label packaging, and to distributors acting as first importers. The core pitch should therefore stay "the converter helps its brand customer," not "the converter needs this for its own compliance." Foodservice converters and distributors are a secondary segment with direct obligations of their own.
- The litigation trend favors the states (Oregon upheld on the merits), which lowers repeal risk. The 9th Circuit appeal and the CO and CA suits still leave tail risk through 2027. A Colorado preliminary injunction for NAW members could temporarily remove some distributors from Colorado obligations.

## Q2. Fee structures: CAA fee schedules, material categories, eco-modulation, typical magnitudes

### Takeaway
Fees are per pound by material category, with roughly 60 categories in Oregon (60–95 across CAA programs). They vary sharply by material and year. Oregon 2026 rates ranged from about 5¢/lb (newsprint) and 8¢/lb (corrugated or paperboard) to 43¢/lb (PE film), 102¢/lb (PP film) and over 100¢/lb (ceramics, EPS). Oregon's 2027 rates fell sharply, partly from a one-time ~$80M reserve return: PE film 12¢, PP film 56¢, clear PET bottles 19¢. California's 2026 Early Fee was trivial at 0.3–2.5¢/lb, but its preliminary 2027 structure adds 26¢/lb for the mitigation fund (the PPMF) on top of base rates for every plastic component. Indicative 2027 CA rates run from about 2–4¢ (glass, corrugated) to $0.88 (LDPE film) and $1.44 (multilayer laminate). Oregon eco-modulation is bonus-only and LCA-based (Bonus A/B/C, capped per SKU). Colorado uses non-LCA bonus and malus factors. The large between-material deltas are exactly what a "swap X for Y" simulator monetizes. The year-to-year volatility is the main modeling challenge.

**Oregon eco-modulation (bonus-only; LCA-based)**
- In the first program cycle, Oregon has three voluntary LCA bonuses and no maluses. Each requires an ISO 14040/14044 LCA with third-party critical review. A SKU can earn only one bonus per year. Bonuses are a percentage of the SKU's own Oregon base fees, applied as credits on 2027 invoices. — [Planet FWD, Bonus A/B/C explainer](https://www.planetfwd.com/blog/blog-oregon-epr-eco-modulation-bonus-a-b-c); [EPR Atlas OR](https://epratlas.com/oregon/)
- Bonus A (disclosure): LCA disclosure on up to 10 SKUs, no impact-reduction requirement. Worth about 10% of the SKU's OR base fees excluding reserves (~8% net), capped at $20K per SKU and $200K per producer. Bonus B (substantial impact reduction): comparative LCA of old versus new design across Oregon's 16-category impact framework, three tiers at roughly 2–2.5× Bonus A, capped at $50K per SKU and $500K per producer. Bonus C (switch to reusable or refillable): capped at $50K per SKU or batch per year. The LCA submission window cited was May 31, 2026. — [Planet FWD](https://www.planetfwd.com/blog/blog-oregon-epr-eco-modulation-bonus-a-b-c); [Planet FWD savings guide](https://www.planetfwd.com/guides/oregon-epr-ecomodulation-savings-guide) (vendor source; caps and tiers should be checked against CAA and DEQ)
- Oregon's largest producers must also submit mandatory life-cycle evaluations on DEQ templates, first due Dec 31, 2026. — [EPR Atlas OR](https://epratlas.com/oregon/) (attribution approximate)

**Colorado**
- Colorado uses bonus and malus eco-modulation without LCA, under 6 CCR 1007-2 Part 1 §18.9. Passive factors are baked into published dues rates, and active credits are applied on top. One source says the four active bonuses are each 1% in rule. Another says bonus benchmarks were revised Oct 2026 and appear only in CAA's login-only portal. — [EPR Atlas CO](https://epratlas.com/colorado/); [eprfeecheck CO](https://eprfeecheck.com/states/colorado); [Packaging School: CO eco-modulation bonuses](https://packagingschool.com/lessons/exploring-colorados-proposed-ecomodulation-bonuses)

It notes official CAA categories run 60–95. — [eprfeecheck CO](https://eprfeecheck.com/states/colorado) (estimates, not official)

**California**
- 2026 Early Fee (published July 20, 2026), six flat rates: glass/ceramics 0.3¢/lb, metal 0.8¢, paper/fiber 0.4¢, rigid plastic 1.3¢, flexible plastic 2.5¢, wood/organic 0.8¢. - Preliminary 2027 structure (Oct 1, 2026, non-binding until CalRecycle approves the plan): a base fee per CMC, plus on plastic components 3¢/lb for reuse investment, 26¢/lb for the PPMF and 0.1¢ per component. — [EPR Atlas CA](https://epratlas.com/california/); [CAA CA Fee-Setting (revised Jun 18, 2026) PDF](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/64260ed078c36925b1cf3385/t/6a34542ebd36443db8b034a5/1781814318407/9_Fee-Setting_CAA_CA_Plan_REVISED_20260618.pdf)
- An aggregator lists indicative draft 2027 CA rates ($/lb): clear PET 0.56; HDPE natural 0.61; PP 0.59; LDPE film 0.88; multilayer laminate 1.44; corrugated 0.04; glass 0.02. — [eprfeecheck, fee rates by state](https://eprfeecheck.com/blog/epr-fee-rates-by-state) (draft or indicative only)
- In the May 2026 illustrative scenarios, EPS foam containers came to 47¢/lb (low) to 127¢/lb (high). CAA said these would change. — [Adept Packaging](https://adeptpackaging.com/blog/california-sb-54-illustrative-fees-are-out-how-to-read-them/); [Western Growers](https://www.wga.com/news/sb-54-estimated-fees-for-california-packaging-regulation/)
- The projected CA 2027 program budget is $1.26B–$1.87B. — [EPR Atlas CA](https://epratlas.com/california/); [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-us/insights/publications/2026/06/californias-epr-program-plan) (attribution approximate)

**General formula**
- Fee = Σ(weight of each material per SKU × state rate for that category) ± eco-modulation adjustments. Spec data (material, weight, recycled content, format) drives the estimate. — [Paper Tube Co: how EPR fees are calculated](https://papertube.co/blogs/down-the-tubes/epr-packaging-fees-calculation); [Packfora](https://packfora.com/blog/epr-packaging-fees-eco-modulation-explained)

### Inferences
- **Illustrative "swap" deltas from the cited rates** (my arithmetic, not sourced claims):
  - Oregon 2027, PP film to PE film: $0.56 − $0.12 = $0.44/lb saved, or about $4,400 per 10,000 lb sold into Oregon. In 2026 the same swap saved $0.59/lb.
  - Oregon 2026, polycoated paperboard (48¢) to uncoated paperboard (8¢): 40¢/lb.
  - Oregon 2027, pigmented to clear PET bottle: $0.34/lb.
  - CA 2027 (indicative), multilayer laminate to LDPE film: about $0.56/lb.
  - CA 2027, any plastic to fiber also avoids the 26¢/lb PPMF surcharge.
  - These are meaningful per pound. Per state they are small for mid-size brands, because Oregon and Colorado are small shares of national sales. California will dominate the dollar value of any simulation from 2027.
- **Volatility is extreme and policy-driven.** Oregon PE film fell 43¢ → 12¢ (−72%) and PP film 102¢ → 56¢ (−45%) between 2026 and 2027, partly from one-time reserve returns. A simulator must version rates by state and year and separate "base" from "reserve drawdown," or it will overstate durable savings.
- The material-category tables are public (OR PDFs, CA early and preliminary schedules). Colorado's eco-modulation benchmarks are reportedly login-only. Rate data is therefore mostly a commodity input, and the value has to come from spec-to-category mapping, eco-modulation logic and multi-state allocation.
- Oregon's LCA bonuses (up to $500K per producer for Bonus B) create a second "simulation" use case beyond material swaps: qualifying redesigns for bonuses. That overlaps with LCA tools like Trayak and Planet FWD.

### Gaps
- Could not open the official CAA PDFs. The 2026 Oregon plastics rates (PET, film, EPS) and the 2027 table columns come from secondary or fragmentary reads.
- Official Colorado 2026 dues schedule (per-category rates) not found. Only estimator ranges are available.
- Official CA 2027 preliminary per-CMC rates (Oct 1, 2026) not retrieved. The aggregator's "draft" figures may predate them.
- Number of CA CMCs not confirmed.
- Exact Oregon Bonus B tier thresholds and the next LCA submission window are not confirmed.
- An "average 17–23¢/lb" Oregon figure appeared in one 2025 analysis but could not be attributed. Omitted.

## Q3. Market size and buyers: converters, brands, producer counts, converter sentiment

### Takeaway
The obvious buyer pool is concentrated. FPA puts US flexible packaging at $42.6B in 2024 sales, with the top 25 converters holding 54% of revenue. Paper converting adds roughly 2,600 locations (about 970 paperboard-container plants), per a commercial business database. CAA had over 2,300 registered producers by about early 2025, 93% obligated in Oregon. California's mandatory PRO enrollment in 2026 likely pushes the brand count well higher. Converters are publicly taking on an "EPR data" role. Sonoco calls it "central," and many converters publish EPR explainers as marketing. But the industry's data standardization effort (the Specright-hosted Sustainable Packaging Data Council) is brand-led, not converter-sold.

### Inferences
- **Buyer count is modest.** In flexible packaging, about 25 converters hold over half of revenue, and the largest (Sonoco, Amcor-type players) are already building in-house data capabilities and joining brand-led councils. The addressable segment for a bought tool is likely mid-size converters, printers and distributors. That could be a few hundred to low thousands of US firms across flexible, paperboard, label, rigid plastic and foodservice. A rough ACV × count check suggests a niche SaaS (single-digit $M ARR potential) unless brands also pay.
- Converters' public EPR content is evidence that they see EPR as a marketing and sales lever. None of the cited converters was found offering an interactive per-customer, per-state fee simulator, though this was not exhaustively checked.
- The brand-led standardization path (SPDC templates, Specright as hub, Lorax as reporting engine) means converters are increasingly being asked to push data into the brand's system. A converter-side tool must export cleanly into those formats rather than compete with them.

Basic per-state fee estimation is already commoditized and largely free: EPR Atlas's per-unit BOM calculator (with redesign comparison), eprfeecheck, rePurpose, extended-producer-responsibility.com, LAPPA and PackEasy. The enterprise stack is Specright (specs) + Lorax EPI (EPR reporting and fees) + Trayak (LCA), and separately Assent + Lorax for supplier-data collection. ### Inferences
- **Simple fee estimates are not a moat.** At least six free or freemium calculators exist, and EPR Atlas already does BOM-level redesign comparison. The defensible pieces for a converter-sold product would be:
  - converter-catalog-to-CAA-category mapping (the converter knows the actual layer structures);
  - multi-tenant sharing (converter → many brand customers, with each brand's state sales mix);
  - quote and RFQ integration (an "EPR cost per 1,000 units" line on quotes);
  - eco-modulation, LCA-bonus and California-PPMF logic;
  - export to SPDC, Specright and Lorax formats, so the brand's compliance stack accepts the data.
- **The most dangerous incumbents for this wedge** are Specright (it already sits between brands and suppliers and hosts the industry data council) and rePurpose (already courting suppliers and building component optimization). Either could add a "supplier portal with fee simulation." Assent's supplier-data network is another plausible entrant.
- I found no evidence that CAA publishes a fee-simulation tool beyond its PDFs and the producer portal. A free CAA-provided estimator would mostly hurt the brand-side "fee estimate" pitch, not the converter-quoting workflow.

## Q5. Business feasibility: willingness to pay, pricing benchmarks, risks

### Takeaway
Pain is rising and money is real: California's 2027 program costs over $1.2B plus a $500M/yr plastics surcharge, and Oregon and Colorado are already invoicing. Converters are demonstrably being pulled into an EPR data role. But no direct evidence was found of converters' willingness to pay for a fee simulator, and no SaaS pricing benchmarks were retrievable. The main risks:
- rapid commoditization by free calculators;
- extreme fee volatility (Oregon film rates moved 45–72% in a year);
- slow rollout in four of seven states (MD, MN and WA fees 2028–2030; Maine stalled);
- the brand-led data standard (Specright/SPDC) pulling the workflow onto the brand side;
- residual litigation (9th Circuit, Colorado, California).

Repeal or delay risk looks lower after Oregon's merits win.

### Inferences
- **Willingness to pay is likely split.** Converters will pay for a sales tool if it wins or defends business, such as RFQs where the brand compares total landed cost including EPR. They are unlikely to pay compliance-software prices for a calculator brands can get free. A plausible model is per-converter seats plus per-active-brand-customer pricing, sold as a sales-enablement tool, not a compliance tool. Mid-size flexible and paperboard converters, plus distributors and private-label suppliers who sometimes are producers, are the likeliest early adopters. This is speculative and no direct evidence was found.
- **Value per brand is often small outside California.** The savings in Q2 are cents per pound, multiplied by Oregon and Colorado volumes, which are small population shares. From 2027, California (with the 26¢/lb plastics surcharge) is what makes "swap X for Y to save $N" material for most brands. A pre-2027 launch should lead with CA preliminary rates, clearly labeled.
- **Data maintenance is the core cost.** At minimum there are 3 fee schedules per year (OR, CO, CA), mid-cycle EQC and CalRecycle changes, reserve drawdowns, and eco-modulation rules (Oregon LCA, Colorado bonus and malus, some in a login-only portal). Accuracy errors would damage the converter's credibility with its customer, which is a reputational risk to the channel partner.
- **Platform risk:** Specright could launch a supplier-side fee simulator inside the SPDC ecosystem, and rePurpose is already building component optimization. Either would erode the wedge. The defensible angle is integration into converter quoting and ERP and estimating systems (e.g., EFI/ePace-type print MIS). That is unverified and would need customer discovery.
- **Litigation:** Oregon's merits win makes outright invalidation less likely. A 9th Circuit reversal or a Colorado preliminary injunction would mainly create carve-outs for NAW members (distributors), not end the programs. Fee-disclosure rules in Colorado could limit how converters present EPR costs on invoices.

### Gaps
- No public pricing benchmarks found for EPR or packaging compliance SaaS (Specright, Lorax EPI, Assent, Sphera, rePurpose, PCX). The search budget was exhausted before pricing searches.
- No survey or interview evidence on converter willingness to pay, or on how often brands request EPR data from converters (e.g., FPA, PMMI or AICC member surveys).
- No evidence collected on CAA plans to offer free estimation or scenario tools.
- Not verified whether the Colorado fee-disclosure prohibition applies to supplier-to-brand B2B quotes. It likely targets consumer point-of-sale charges, but this needs legal review.
- Not confirmed whether print MIS or ERP vendors serving converters (e.g., EFI, Cerm, Label Traxx) have added EPR fields or calculators. This is a potential competitor or partner channel.
