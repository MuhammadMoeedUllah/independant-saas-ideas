## 1. Data acquisition: sources/APIs, cost, coverage, refresh, and how aggregators republish postings

### Takeaway
You can buy a cheap, credit-based "job postings" feed (TheirStack, Techmap, PredictLeads, Coresignal, JobsPikr, DataForSEO/SerpApi for Google Jobs) for roughly $0.001–$0.04 per record. No single feed covers "everywhere a posting appears." The most accurate and cheapest MVP path is to pull the employer's own ATS public endpoints first (Greenhouse and Lever expose structured pay-range and workplace-type fields with no auth), then sample third-party boards with vendor feeds or targeted scraping. Google Jobs scraping is currently unreliable.

### Inferences
- **Recommended MVP data stack (cheapest per signal):** (a) Detect the employer's ATS from career-site links (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, Workday, iCIMS, etc.) and pull **structured** pay and workplace fields from public endpoints. This is free, deterministic, and the canonical "source of truth." (b) Add one credit-based aggregator feed (TheirStack or Techmap) to find copies on boards. (c) Use DataForSEO or SerpApi for Google Jobs SERP sampling only where needed. (d) Run targeted, low-volume, logged-off checks of specific posting URLs on Indeed, LinkedIn, ZipRecruiter, and Glassdoor rather than bulk crawling.
- Unit economics look favorable. At about $0.0015–$0.01 per record, monitoring a 200-requisition employer across about 10 surfaces daily (roughly 60k record-checks per month) costs on the order of $100–$600/mo in data COGS. Deduplicating and diffing only changed postings would cut that sharply. This is my estimate, not vendor-quoted.
- Vendor feeds are built for labor-market analytics, not compliance evidence. They give you "a posting exists on board X." They do not reliably give you "a screenshot/HTML of exactly what board X displayed at time T." The product still needs its own fetch-and-archive step (hash plus timestamp) for each flagged URL to produce defensible evidence.
- Coresignal's LinkedIn-only sourcing and LinkUp's employer-site-only sourcing are complementary. Neither one by itself finds "stale reposts on scraper sites," which is the core differentiated value.

### Gaps
- **Lightcast, Revelio Labs, Bright Data datasets/Scraper APIs**: I got no pricing or coverage data because the search budget ran out. From background knowledge (unverified this session): Lightcast and Revelio are enterprise contracts, typically five to six figures per year; Bright Data sells per-record datasets and scraper APIs for LinkedIn, Indeed, and Glassdoor. Verify on vendor pages.
- **Indeed/LinkedIn partner APIs**: unverified. From background knowledge, Indeed retired its public Publisher Job Search API years ago, and LinkedIn's Job Posting API is limited to approved ATS/job-distribution partners. Neither offers employers a "find all copies of my posting" API.

- **How "job wrapping" and XML feeds work** (ATS to Indeed/LinkedIn/ZipRecruiter feeds; aggregators such as Jooble, Talent.com, and Adzuna ingesting XML feeds or crawling career pages; programmatic job ad networks such as Appcast and Joveo redistributing): searches on this were blocked. ## 2. Legality of crawling: case law, ToS constraints, and reliability of schema.org JobPosting signals

### Takeaway
Logged-off scraping of public job pages is defensible in the Ninth Circuit/N.D. Cal. line of cases (Meta v. Bright Data, Jan 2024; X Corp v. Bright Data, May 2024). Those rulings are narrow and fact-specific, though: logged-in scraping, server-impairment, and fraud-based UCL theories survive. The product should stay logged-off, rate-limited, and evidence-focused. Schema.org `baseSalary` is useful but not reliable by itself, and `estimatedSalary` must **not** be treated as employer disclosure.

### Cited Findings
- **Meta v. Bright Data (N.D. Cal., Judge Chen, Jan. 2024):** summary judgment for Bright Data on breach of contract. The court held that Meta's terms did not prohibit **logged-off scraping of public data**, reasoning that a non-account scraper "stands in the same shoes as a visitor to whom the Terms cannot apply." The ruling is limited to Meta's ToS. It did not decide logged-in scraping because Meta offered insufficient evidence that Bright Data scraped while logged in. — [Quinn Emanuel client alert (Jan 2024)](https://www.quinnemanuel.com/the-firm/news-events/client-alert-what-does-the-meta-v-bright-data-summary-judgment-ruling-mean-for-web-scraping/); [Farella Braun + Martel analysis](https://www.fbm.com/technology/publications/major-decision-affects-law-of-scraping-and-online-data-collection-meta-platforms-v-bright-data/)
- **X Corp v. Bright Data (N.D. Cal.):** dismissed with leave to amend on May 9, 2024. The court held that ToS-based scraping claims were **conflict-preempted by the Copyright Act**. On Nov. 26, 2024, X was allowed to add a **fraud-based UCL theory** and **access-based breach claims (impairment of X's servers)**, but scraping-based breach claims were not revived. Bright Data answered and counterclaimed, including antitrust, on Dec. 17, 2024. I found **no Ninth Circuit appeal** in this case. — [Proskauer New Media & Technology Law blog (May 14 2024)](https://newmedialaw.proskauer.com/2024/05/14/california-court-issues-another-noteworthy-decision-dismissing-breach-of-contract-and-tort-claims-in-web-scraping-dispute/); [Skadden on copyright preemption (May 2024)](https://skadden.com/insights/publications/2024/05/district-court-adopts-broad-view); [Eric Goldman blog guest post (Jan 2025)](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/archives/2025/01/catching-up-on-the-heavyweight-scraping-battle-between-x-and-bright-data-guest-blog-post.htm)
- ATS vendors explicitly treat published postings as public. Lever's docs say published postings "may be scraped by third parties," and Greenhouse's docs say Job Board GETs require no authentication. — [lever/postings-api](https://github.com/lever/postings-api); [grnhse/greenhouse-api-docs](https://github.com/grnhse/greenhouse-api-docs)
- JobSpy's LinkedIn scraper uses the logged-off "jobs-guest" endpoint. Its README warns "All of the job board sites are aggressive with blocking" and recommends proxies. — [JobSpy](https://github.com/speedyapply/JobSpy)
- **schema.org definitions:** `baseSalary` is "The base salary of the job." `estimatedSalary` is "An estimated salary… often computed by outside organizations rather than the hiring organization, who may not have committed to the estimated value." `jobBenefits` is "Description of benefits associated with the job." `jobLocationType` is "e.g. TELECOMMUTE for telecommute jobs." `validThrough` is the date after which the item is not valid. — [schemaorg/schemaorg data/schema.ttl](https://github.com/schemaorg/schemaorg)

### Inferences
- **Practical legal posture for the product:** scrape only logged-off, publicly accessible pages. Never create accounts or accept board ToS by logging in. Honor rate limits and back off on 429s. Do not circumvent technical blocks (CAPTCHAs, IP bans) with deception. Store minimal content: the posting text and screenshot needed as evidence for the customer's own posting. Prefer licensed feeds (TheirStack, LinkUp, etc.) for discovery, so the vendor carries the collection risk, and limit your own fetching to confirming specific URLs.
- The customer is the **owner of the underlying posting content**. Monitoring copies of its own job ads is a sympathetic use case compared with commercial scraping of third-party user data. That helps with ToS/UCL optics but is not a legal safe harbor.
- **The schema signal is useful but insufficient.** Aggregators and boards may show estimated pay (Indeed and Glassdoor "estimated salary" widgets are an example). A compliance engine must distinguish "employer-provided range" from "board-estimated range." Schema.org formalizes that distinction (`baseSalary` vs `estimatedSalary`), but sites may mislabel it. The employer is the one liable for the disclosure, so a board estimate should be scored as **missing** employer disclosure.

### Gaps
- **hiQ v. LinkedIn final outcome**: not verified this session. From background knowledge (unverified): after the Ninth Circuit's 2022 remand ruling (post-Van Buren) that CFAA "without authorization" likely does not reach public pages, the district court in Nov. 2022 found hiQ breached LinkedIn's User Agreement (fake accounts/logged-in scraping). The case ended in a Dec. 2022 consent judgment with a $500k payment and destruction of scraped data. Lesson: **contract claims and logged-in/fake-account scraping** are the real risk.
- **Van Buren v. United States (2021)**: unverified this session. Background: the Supreme Court adopted a "gates-up-or-down" reading of "exceeds authorized access," narrowing CFAA's reach for public-facing data. Confirm the citation (593 U.S. 374).
- Each board's current ToS (Indeed, LinkedIn, ZipRecruiter, Glassdoor) on automated access was not checked.
- Google's JobPosting **structured-data guidelines** (`applicantLocationRequirements`, `directApply`, pay-range guidance) could not be fetched; those two properties are not in the core schema.org vocabulary file I checked. I found no study measuring how often `baseSalary` in JSON-LD matches visible posting text.

## 3. Parsing and compliance logic: extraction difficulty, remote/hybrid detection, jurisdiction mapping, rule differences, and open datasets

### Takeaway
Extracting a numeric range is easy for structured ATS fields and moderately hard from free text; naive regex like JobSpy's misses a lot. The hard part is **jurisdiction applicability for remote/hybrid roles**. Several statutes (CA, IL, NY, CT, MA) reach out-of-state or remote roles based on facts **not present in the posting**, such as a reporting supervisor's location or whether the job "could be filled" in-state. The engine therefore needs employer-supplied metadata plus a conservative default. Rules differ materially on benefits disclosure, hyperlinks, open-ended ranges, thresholds, and cure periods. I found no maintained open dataset of these rules; trackers are vendor or law-firm web pages.

### Cited Findings
**Extraction and remote-detection baselines (primary code)**
- JobSpy's description salary parser takes "The first '$min - $max' of a text" using a single regex (`\$(\d+…)([kK]?)\s*[-—–]\s*(?:\$)?(\d+…)([kK]?)`). It guesses the interval from magnitude (below 350 = hourly, below 30,000 = monthly) and discards annualized values outside $1,000–$700,000. It records `salary_source` as `direct_data` vs `description` (parsed from text). Its remote flag is a keyword match on title plus location only. — [JobSpy util.py and linkedin/util.py (v1.3.0)](https://github.com/speedyapply/JobSpy)
- Greenhouse can return **multiple location-specific ranges** per post (example title "NYC Salary Range"), and Lever exposes `workplaceType` (`remote`/`hybrid`/`on-site`). For ATS-sourced postings, both range and remote status can be read deterministically. — [Greenhouse docs](https://github.com/grnhse/greenhouse-api-docs); [Lever postings-api](https://github.com/lever/postings-api)

**Rule differences the engine must encode (status as of 2026-10-09; from law-firm summaries)**
- **California (Lab. Code §432.3):** 15+ employees; pay scale must be **in the posting itself**. The Labor Commissioner reportedly says a **link or QR code does not satisfy** this. Covers "any position that could be filled by a worker in California, including fully remote roles." If a staffing agency, recruiter, or job board posts on the employer's behalf, the employer **must give it the pay scale and the third party must include it** (blog cites §432.3(c)(5); statute subsection not verified). **SB 642** (effective Jan 1, 2026) redefines "pay scale" as a "good faith estimate" of the range the employer reasonably expects to pay "upon hire" and extends the statute of limitations to 3 years. — [California Employment Law Report (Apr 2026)](https://www.californiaemploymentlawreport.com/2026/04/what-california-law-requires-in-your-job-postings/); [Morgan Lewis (Nov 2025)](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2025/11/california-amends-pay-transparency-requirements)
- **Colorado (EPEWA Part 2):** the current guidance is **INFO #9A (May 29, 2024)**, which relies on the POST Rules (7 CCR 1103-18). The 2021 INFO #9 says compliance is not required by "a site or publication that, on its own or when hired by an employer with a job open, just posts another employer's jobs." It also says it is "the employer's responsibility to assure continuous compliance with functionality of links, up-to-date information, and information that applies to the specific job posting," and that one range cannot be reused across jobs with different actual pay. — [CDLE INFO #9A (05.29.24) PDF](https://cdle.colorado.gov/sites/cdle/files/info_%239a_transparency_in_pay_and_job_opportunities_the_colorado_epewa_part_2_05.29.24.pdf); [CDLE INFO #9 (2021) via CBA](https://Www.Cobar.org/Portals/COBAR/Repository/CBA/10322/CDLE%20INFO9_%20Equal%20Pay%20Transparency%20Rules%20(2021).pdf); [ezel.ai mirror of INFO #9A](https://ezel.ai/labor-guidance/co/info-9a)
- **Washington (RCW 49.58.110, as amended by SSB 5408, effective July 27, 2025):** any person may give written notice of a noncompliant posting. If the employer **corrects within 5 business days, no damages or penalties** may be assessed. During the cure, the employer must also contact third-party posting entities to correct the posting. Employers are not liable for **unauthorized third-party postings**, for example when a third party scrapes and reposts without authorization. Statutory damages changed from a flat amount to **$100–$5,000 per violation**, and a single fixed pay figure is allowed where only one amount is offered. One summary says the cure grace period expires **July 27, 2027** (verify). — [Davis Wright Tremaine (Apr 2025)](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/employment-labor-and-benefits/2025/04/wa-wage-transparency-equal-pay-act-amended); [Littler](https://www.littler.com/news-analysis/asap/relief-steep-pay-transparency-penalties-businesses-washington-state); [Ogletree](https://ogletree.com/insights-resources/blog-posts/washington-governor-signs-bill-making-key-changes-to-equal-pay-and-opportunities-act/); [Ashurst Perkins Coie](https://www.ashurstperkinscoie.com/en/insights/washington-state-amends-equal-pay-law-to-clarify-employer-requirements-and-liability/); [Morgan Lewis (May 2025)](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2025/05/washington-state-clarifies-its-pay-transparency-law)
  - **Branson v. Washington Fine Wine & Spirits** (Wash. Sup. Ct., Sept. 4, 2025, 5 Wash. 3d 289): a "job applicant" need not be a bona fide applicant, so "whether an applicant had a genuine interest in the position is immaterial." This exposes employers to "tester" plaintiffs. — [K&L Gates (Sept 8 2025)](https://www.klgates.com/Washington-Supreme-Court-Adopts-Expansive-Definition-of-Job-Applicant-Under-EPOA-Pay-Transparency-Statute-9-8-2025); [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/hunton-employment-labor-perspectives/the-washington-supreme-courts-decision-in-branson-v-washington-fine-wine-spirits-llc-and-its-significance-for-employers); [Williams Kastner](https://www.williamskastner.com/news-insights/article/washington-supreme-court-expands-exposure-under-pay-transparency-law-retail-hospitality-employers/)
- **New York State (Lab. Law §194-b):** 4+ employees. Covers jobs performed at least in part in NY, and reportedly jobs that report to a NY supervisor, office, or worksite. The range is the min/max the employer "believes in good faith is accurate." Under the **proposed** regulations (2023), an overly broad range is evidence of bad faith, and ranges must be per single opportunity and location, so multi-location ads need multiple ranges. The employer must keep the history of ranges and job descriptions. Penalties: $1,000, $2,000, then $3,000. — [Tully Rinckey](https://www.tullylegal.com/resources/articles/pay-transparency-law-goes-into-effect-across-new-york-state/); [Littler on proposed NY regs](https://www.littler.com/news-analysis/asap/new-york-state-proposes-regulations-pay-transparency-job-advertisements); [Mintz (Dec 2022)](https://mintz.com/insights-center/viewpoints/2226/2022-12-23-new-york-state-enacts-pay-transparency-law)
- **Illinois (Equal Pay Act amendment, effective Jan 1, 2025):** 15+ employees. Covers jobs performed at least partly in IL **or reporting to an IL supervisor/office**; occasional IL visits don't count. Must include the pay scale plus a **general description of benefits and other compensation** (bonuses, stock options, incentives). **Open-ended ranges ("$40,000 and up," "up to $60,000") are not acceptable.** A **hyperlink** to a public page with pay and benefits is allowed. If out-of-state pay differs, show the IL range. The employer must give pay and benefits (or a link) to third-party boards. **A third party is liable unless it shows the employer failed to provide the info.** Internal promotion notice is due within 14 days. **Records of pay scale, benefits, and each posting must be kept 5 years.** After notice, the employer has 7 days to remedy before a $100/day civil penalty, and each posting is a separate violation. — [Seyfarth on IDOL FAQ](https://www.seyfarth.com/news-insights/illinois-department-of-labor-provides-long-awaited-transparency-to-equal-pay-act-amendments.html); [Jackson Lewis 10 takeaways](https://www.jacksonlewis.com/insights/10-key-takeaways-employers-new-illinois-pay-transparency-job-posting-law); [Greenberg Traurig (Dec 2024)](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2024/12/new-year-new-rules-illinois-mandates-pay-transparency-requirements-in-2025); [Amundsen Davis](https://www.amundsendavislaw.com/labor-employment-law-update/illinois-department-of-labor-gives-long-awaited-guidance-on-january-1-2025-pay-transparency-requirements)
- **Massachusetts (effective Oct 29, 2025):** 25+ employees whose primary place of work is MA, including remote employees who telecommute into or live in MA (the AG uses the Earned Sick Time "primary place of work" test). The range must be what the employer "reasonably and in good faith expects to pay." **Benefits and bonuses are generally excluded** (commission ranges are possibly required). Covers third-party and recruiter postings. — [Mintz (Sep 30 2025)](https://www.mintz.com/insights-center/viewpoints/2226/2025-09-30-massachusetts-pay-transparency-law-key-updates-regarding); [Greenberg Traurig (Oct 2025)](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2025/10/massachusetts-pay-transparency-law-takes-effect-on-oct-29-2025); [Holland & Knight (Sep 24 2025)](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/09/a-reminder-of-pay-transparency-requirements-taking-effect)
- **New Jersey (effective June 1, 2025):** 10+ employees over 20 calendar weeks. Must show the wage or range **plus benefits and other compensation programs the employee would be eligible for in the first 12 months**. Covers internal promotions. Penalties are $300 for a first violation and $600 thereafter, enforced by the Commissioner, with no private right of action noted. ### Inferences
- **Rule-engine schema (minimum fields per jurisdiction):** effective date; employer-size threshold and how it's counted (in-state vs total); nexus test (performed in-state / could be filled in-state / reports to in-state supervisor / primary place of work); required elements (range; benefits description; other compensation; commission/tips language); range-quality rules (no open-ended ranges; good-faith/"upon hire"; per-location ranges); hyperlink allowed? (IL yes, CA no); internal postings covered?; third-party duty allocation (employer must supply, third party must include, third party liable unless employer failed, unauthorized scrapes excluded); cure window (WA 5 business days, VA 15 business days, IL 7 days post-notice); penalty schedule; record-retention period (IL 5 years, ME employment + 3 years, NY unspecified).
- **Remote/hybrid mapping can't be solved from posting text alone.** CA's "could be filled by a worker in CA" means any US-remote posting is in scope for CA. IL, CT, and NY add reporting-line nexus. MA uses "primary place of work." The engine should (1) treat "Remote – US" as triggering the strictest union (CA, CO, WA, NY, IL, MN, NJ, VT, MA, VA, ME, CT…) and (2) let the employer attach `reporting_location` metadata per requisition to narrow scope. That is a product moat, because pure scrapers can't know reporting lines.
- **Benefits detection is the under-served check.** IL, MN, NJ, CT, WA (per background), and DE require benefits descriptions; CA and VT do not; MA excludes them. A simple classifier for a "benefits section present" (health, retirement, PTO, 401(k) tokens) plus link-liveness checks (CO and IL make the employer responsible for working links) is cheap and high-value.
- **Regex vs LLM:** JobSpy's single-regex approach misses hourly ranges with "/hr," "to" separators, unicode spaces, multiple ranges per location, and "DOE"/open-ended phrasing. An LLM extraction step with JSON-schema output, validated against ATS ground truth from Greenhouse/Lever structured fields, is the obvious MVP approach. ATS structured data doubles as a free labeled evaluation set (my inference; no benchmark found).

### Gaps
- **Washington's base content requirement** was not re-verified this session. Background (unverified): RCW 49.58.110 requires the wage scale or salary range **and a general description of all benefits and other compensation** in postings for employers with 15+ employees.
- **Colorado's specific 2024 additions** (application-deadline disclosure and post-selection notification under SB 23-105 / POST Rules) were not verified this session. Background (unverified): postings must state the expected application deadline, and employers must make reasonable efforts to remove or update postings for filled roles.
- **Maryland (Oct 1, 2024), DC (June 30, 2024), Hawaii (Jan 1, 2024), NYC, Jersey City, Ithaca, Westchester, Cleveland (Oct 27, 2025), Columbus (2027)**: none verified this session.
- **No quantitative NLP accuracy benchmark** for pay-range extraction from postings was found, because searches were exhausted. Background (unverified): Hansen, Lambert, Bloom, Davis, Sadun & Taska (NBER w31007, 2023) built a fine-tuned language model ("WHAM") classifying remote-work availability in Lightcast postings at about 99% accuracy vs human labels. Verify before citing.

## 4. Takedown/correction mechanics: aggregator processes, agency guidance on third-party postings, and the value of a timestamped log

### Takeaway
State law now **explicitly rewards fast, documented correction**: WA has a 5-business-day cure after written notice, VA a 15-business-day cure "on the original posting locations," and IL 7 days after notice. Record-keeping duties (IL 5 years, NY range history, ME employment + 3 years) make a **timestamped notice-to-correction log** directly relevant evidence. Third-party liability splits by state: CO historically exempted mere reposting sites, WA excludes unauthorized scrapes, IL shifts liability to boards that had the data, and CA puts a hand-off duty on the employer. I could not verify the aggregators' own employer correction and removal processes this session.

### Cited Findings
- **WA:** written notice from any person starts a 5-business-day cure. Correcting within the window means "no damages or penalties." The employer must also contact third-party posting entities. Employers are not liable for unauthorized third-party scrapes and reposts. Commentators advise a **designated email address in postings to receive notices** and **pre-identified contacts at third-party posting vendors**. The statute does not specify how notice is delivered. — [DWT](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/employment-labor-and-benefits/2025/04/wa-wage-transparency-equal-pay-act-amended); [Fox Rothschild](https://www.foxrothschild.com/publications/washington-state-may-ease-pay-transparency-rules); [Jackson Lewis](https://www.jacksonlewis.com/index%2Ephp/node/31771); [NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/article/washington-state-makes-key-changes-amend-equal-pay-and-opportunities-act)
- **VA:** "If the employer corrects the posting on the original posting locations within 15 business days of receiving such notice, no action for a violation shall be brought." — [Morgan Lewis (May 2026)](https://morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/05/virginia-law-will-require-pay-transparency-restrict-employers-from-seeking-wage-history); [DLA Piper](https://knowledge.dlapiper.com/dlapiperknowledge/globalemploymentlatestdevelopments/2026/virginia-pay-transparency-law-goes-into-effect-july-1-2026)
- **IL:** the employer must supply pay and benefits (or a link) to third-party boards such as LinkedIn and Indeed. The third party is liable unless it shows the employer failed to provide them. The employer has 7 days after notice to remedy before a $100/day penalty applies. Records of each posting must be kept 5 years. — [Seyfarth](https://www.seyfarth.com/news-insights/illinois-department-of-labor-provides-long-awaited-transparency-to-equal-pay-act-amendments.html); [Greenberg Traurig](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2024/12/new-year-new-rules-illinois-mandates-pay-transparency-requirements-in-2025)
- **CA:** the employer must give the pay scale to the third party, and the third party must include it. Commentary says employers "cannot shift this duty to a vendor by assumption" and should confirm the range is actually published on each platform. — [California Employment Law Report (Apr 2026)](https://www.californiaemploymentlawreport.com/2026/04/what-california-law-requires-in-your-job-postings/)
- **CO:** the 2021 INFO #9 exempts sites that "just post[] another employer's jobs," even when hired by the employer. The employer remains responsible for working links and up-to-date info. The current guidance is INFO #9A (May 29, 2024). — [CDLE INFO #9 (2021)](https://Www.Cobar.org/Portals/COBAR/Repository/CBA/10322/CDLE%20INFO9_%20Equal%20Pay%20Transparency%20Rules%20(2021).pdf); [CDLE INFO #9A](https://cdle.colorado.gov/sites/cdle/files/info_%239a_transparency_in_pay_and_job_opportunities_the_colorado_epewa_part_2_05.29.24.pdf)
- **MA:** guidance says to give updated postings to any third-party recruiters posting on the employer's behalf. **MN, VT, and ME** cover postings made through third parties. — [Mintz](https://www.mintz.com/insights-center/viewpoints/2226/2025-09-30-massachusetts-pay-transparency-law-key-updates-regarding); [Seyfarth MN](https://seyfarth.com/news-insights/minnesota-enacts-pay-transparency-law-to-require-salary-range-disclosure-in-job-postings.html); [Greenberg Traurig VT](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2024/6/vermont-joins-pay-transparency-trend-with-new-job-posting-law); [Trusaic ME](https://trusaic.com/blog/maine-pay-transparency-law-set-to-take-effect-july-29-2026/)
- **Record-keeping hooks:** NY requires keeping the history of ranges and job descriptions per advertised opportunity, with no duration specified. ME requires records for employment plus 3 years. — [Littler NY](https://www.littler.com/news-analysis/asap/new-york-state-proposes-regulations-pay-transparency-job-advertisements); [Pierce Atwood](https://www.pierceatwood.com/alerts/new-wage-disclosure-obligations-maine)

### Inferences
- **Correction-log design should mirror the cure statutes:** (1) notice received (who, channel, timestamp, posting URL); (2) business-day clock per jurisdiction (WA 5, VA 15, IL 7 calendar days post-IDOL notice); (3) source posting corrected (ATS diff with before/after hash); (4) each third-party copy, with the request sent (email or form, timestamp, recipient), the board's response, and a re-crawl verification screenshot; (5) unauthorized copies flagged as "not employer-authorized" (WA defense) with evidence of no feed relationship. This is evidence the employer acted within statutory windows, which is the strongest available "defense value" claim. No court decision applying a log was found.
- **Product insight:** under WA and VA, the clock starts at **someone else's** notice. A scanner that finds problems **before** a tester does (and auto-routes fixes to the ATS so feeds propagate) is the core value. The log is the backup.
- **Third-party allocation drives what the product asks boards to do.** In IL, telling a board "we supplied the range, see attached" shifts liability to the board. In CA, the duty to hand off is the employer's. In WA, unauthorized scrapes aren't the employer's liability, but authorized syndication partners must be contacted. The takedown module should therefore classify each copy as *owned* (ATS career page), *authorized syndication* (paid or free feed, programmatic partner), or *unauthorized scrape/repost*, and choose a template per jurisdiction.

### Gaps
- **Aggregator employer correction and removal processes** (Indeed, LinkedIn, ZipRecruiter, Glassdoor, Adzuna, Jooble, Talent.com, SimplyHired; Google for Jobs via `validThrough`, Indexing API `URL_DELETED`, or removing the page) were **not researched** because the search budget ran out. Background (unverified): most boards index from the employer's career site or XML feed, so fixing the source and feed propagates within days. Boards offer "report a job"/employer-support forms for unauthorized copies. Google drops expired postings when the page 404s, the structured data is removed, or `validThrough` passes. Each needs primary-source confirmation, and the status of SimplyHired and Glassdoor's jobs product after Recruit's consolidation should be checked.
- Whether WA's L&I administrative policy (ES.E.1 / pay transparency guidance) has been updated post-SSB 5408 on third-party job boards was not checked.
- I found **no case or agency statement** saying a timestamped monitoring log reduces liability; the value is inferred from cure and record-keeping statutes.
- WA class-action volume (pre/post SSB 5408) was not quantified.

## 6. Build effort: MVP components, comparables, and open-source scrapers and their limits

### Takeaway
A credible MVP is a **2–3 month build for a small team** (my estimate). It combines an ATS connector for Greenhouse and Lever (public, structured), a board-discovery layer (licensed feed plus JobSpy-style targeted fetches), LLM extraction, a jurisdiction rules engine encoded from about 15 states, an evidence archiver, and a correction workflow. JobSpy is a usable prototype scraper (MIT, actively maintained, v1.3.0 on 2026-10-08) but is not production-grade for compliance evidence. It is rate-limited, caps out at about 1,000 results per search, does not currently work for Google Jobs, and uses simplistic salary and remote parsing.

### Cited Findings
- **JobSpy / python-jobspy:** MIT license; 84 PyPI releases from 0.1.0 (2023-09-03) to 1.3.0 (2026-10-08). Scrapes LinkedIn, Indeed, Glassdoor, ZipRecruiter, Bayt, Naukri, BDJobs, and HelloWork, plus a Google option. README limits: "Indeed is the best scraper currently with no rate limiting"; "Most job board endpoints are capped at around 1000 jobs on a given search"; "LinkedIn rate limits bursts of requests with a 429"; "Glassdoor rate limits above about 2 requests a second"; "Google Jobs is currently unavailable"; "All of the job board sites are aggressive with blocking." The output schema includes `job_url_direct` (useful for linking a board copy back to the ATS source), `salary_source`, `min_amount`/`max_amount`, `is_remote`, `work_from_home_type`, and `emails`. — [speedyapply/JobSpy README and source](https://github.com/speedyapply/JobSpy); [PyPI python-jobspy](https://pypi.org/project/python-jobspy/)
- **Structured ATS sources:** Greenhouse (`pay_transparency=true` returns `pay_input_ranges`, no auth for GETs) and Lever (`salaryRange`, `workplaceType`, published postings public). — [Greenhouse docs](https://github.com/grnhse/greenhouse-api-docs); [Lever postings-api](https://github.com/lever/postings-api)
- **Licensed discovery feeds** at low entry price: TheirStack (free 200 credits, $59/mo entry), Techmap (about $1 per 1k postings), DataForSEO Google Jobs ($0.0006 per SERP page). — see section 1 sources
- Existing "agency guidance as structured data" products exist (ezel.ai labor-guidance pages with active/superseded status). — [ezel.ai INFO #9A](https://ezel.ai/labor-guidance/co/info-9a)

### Inferences
- **MVP components (suggested order):**
  1. **Employer onboarding:** domain, ATS detection, requisition list with `reporting_location` and `remote_eligibility` overrides.
  2. **Source-of-truth ingest:** Greenhouse and Lever first, then Ashby, Workday, iCIMS, and SmartRecruiters.
  3. **Copy discovery:** TheirStack/Techmap search by company; JobSpy-style targeted queries on Indeed, LinkedIn, ZipRecruiter, and Glassdoor; DataForSEO for Google Jobs; fuzzy-match on title, location, and description hash; stale-repost detection (copy live after the ATS req closed).
  4. **Extraction:** structured fields first, then LLM JSON extraction of range, interval, currency, benefits presence, remote/hybrid, commission/tips language, and hyperlinks; then link-liveness checks.
  5. **Rules engine:** versioned YAML/JSON per jurisdiction with effective dates (section 3 schema), producing per-posting findings and a severity score (penalty schedule × private-right-of-action × number of copies × days live).
  6. **Evidence archive:** HTML, screenshot, SHA-256, and fetch timestamp; immutable storage.
  7. **Remediation:** ATS fix suggestions; templated correction and takedown emails or forms per board; a business-day cure timer per jurisdiction; re-verification crawl.
  8. **Reporting:** counsel-ready export of the correction log.
- **Biggest technical risks:** (a) board anti-bot blocking at scale (JobSpy's README itself warns of it), so lean on licensed feeds for breadth; (b) false positives where boards show estimated pay or truncate descriptions, so always compare to the source posting and use `baseSalary` vs `estimatedSalary` semantics; (c) jurisdiction ambiguity for remote roles, so default to conservative scope plus employer metadata.
- The defensibility is not the scraping. It is the **versioned rules corpus, the cure-clock workflow, and the evidence log**, plus integrations, because scraping itself is commoditized (JobSpy, cheap feeds).

### Gaps
- No public examples of comparable pay-transparency monitoring products "built quickly" were found; not researched.
- Other OSS options (Crawlee, Scrapy, Playwright-based ATS scrapers, JobFunnel) were not evaluated. I only confirmed that the `apify/crawlee` and `PaulMcInnis/JobFunnel` repos exist via `git ls-remote`; maintenance status is unknown.
- Workday and iCIMS public-endpoint structures and their pay fields were not verified.
