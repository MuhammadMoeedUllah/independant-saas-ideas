# Pre-Sell Three Wedges, Then Build One

None of the 106 ideas in the SaaS-Opportunity-Research corpus is a sure thing for a solo developer who needs US revenue soon, but five survive fresh web validation as **"build-if" bets**, worth building only after buyers pre-pay, and the strongest is a narrow tool built around a California law that took effect nine days ago rather than any of the corpus's own favorites. In rank order the survivors are **K02 "CARS Act 3-Day Log"** for California used-car lots, **B04 cut down to a no-telephony "Lead Ledger"** that shows contractors their cost per booked job by lead source, **F06 narrowed to an "ESA Receivables Ledger"** for private schools paid through state education savings accounts, **S1-01**, an April 15 cybersecurity filing kit for small New York insurance agencies, and, at low confidence, **B07**, a HoneyBook exit kit for photographers; C05's CoConstruct exit kit merits only a short pre-sold cash project, and H05, K03, I03, E04 and S2-01 should not be built. On labeled assumptions, typical paths to **$5k MRR run 6–24 months**, with K02 fastest at **6–9 months at $59/mo**, and every survivor has a concrete way to fail before code is written. The founder's first move, starting Monday Oct 12 2026, is **three parallel smoke tests** (K02, B04-narrow and F06), each with a landing page, a free calculator, 300–1,000 outreach contacts and a founding pre-sale offer, followed by a single build decision on **Friday Nov 6**, a mid-December ship and a target of **10–20 paying customers by Jan 10 2027**. Building with Claude cuts each MVP to 4–6 weeks and makes lead magnets nearly free, but it does not shorten the phone calls, visits and cold emails that set the revenue pace. Most web facts here come from search-result summaries because direct page fetches were blocked, the revenue models rest on labeled assumptions with no customer interviews yet, and the corpus itself is third-party research written Sep 24–25 2026.

## The corpus's favorites lost points on parity, telephony and cheap rivals

The corpus ranked each idea out of 25 on pain, price gap, build ease, sales speed and market size, plus a bonus for every state that ranked it top-3, a frame that suits a funded team replacing a bloated incumbent. The re-screen applied the founder's own rubric instead: **eight criteria scored 1–5 for a total out of 40** (solo build scope, regulatory burden, self-serve sale, distribution one person can reach, willingness to pay, gap versus cheap indie tools, urgency between Oct 2026 and Apr 2027, and support load), **eight kill flags** such as HIPAA, payments at the core, texting that needs carrier registration at scale, products that must match an incumbent feature for feature, and markets crowded with cheap tools, and a **wedge rule** demanding the narrowest product a buyer would pay for on its own that ships in 4–6 weeks ([rubric](../research_notes/Solo%20developer%20SaaS%20opportunity%20shortlist/_solo_dev_rubric.md)). Five corpus-only screens covered all 106 ideas (19 horizontal tools; 22 trades, hospitality and auto; 21 health, wellness and property; 21 professional, nonprofit and compliance; 23 education and state-specific), and the eleven strongest wedges then went through a web-validation round on Oct 10 2026 with roughly 35–40 searches each. Every rubric score in this report is one analyst's judgment.

The corpus's #1 idea, **B04 FirstCall** (missed-call text-back and an AI lead qualifier for home-service contractors, 23/25, top-3 in 40 of 51 states), scored only **27/40** because its core value depends on SMS carrier registration, phone support and TCPA exposure the corpus never priced ([screen notes](../research_notes/Solo%20developer%20SaaS%20opportunity%20shortlist/screen_group1_horizontal_tools.md)). Validation then found the category saturated. Jobber launched an AI Receptionist on Aug 25 2025 ([DestinationCRM](https://www.destinationcrm.com/Articles/CRM-News/CRM-Across-the-Wire/Jobber-Launches-Receptionist-171139.aspx)), reportedly a **$99 add-on** ([PipelineOn](https://pipelineon.com/blog/ai-receptionist-contractor/)); Housecall Pro already pulls Angi leads into its inbox natively ([HCP help](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/9657761-job-inbox-integration-with-angi-leads)); LeadTruffle sells the same bundle at $229–629/mo ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/10035919/LeadTruffle/)); and GoHighLevel agencies resell missed-call text-back on a $97 stack ([SupplyGem](https://supplygem.com/gohighlevel-missed-call-text-back/)). Carrier approval for business texting takes 1–4 weeks per one 2026 guide ([Conduit](https://www.conduit.ai/blog/a2p-10dlc-registration-step-by-step-for-2026)) and up to 6 per another ([Zonitel](https://a2pregistration.zonitel.com/)), which rules out a same-day free trial. **A03 LocalLoop** (22/25, a Podium/Birdeye replacement) fell to 24/40 on the same SMS dependency, a GoHighLevel bundle at $97, and revenue that arrives only when each prospect's contract ends; it survived only as a possible second module on B04 and was not validated further.

**F01 Chairly**, the salon-booking idea the corpus ranked #3 of 106 and named "Bet 3" (22/25), fell to 24/40 because its planned $19 plan competes with Fresha at $19.95 ([Fresha](https://www.fresha.com/pricing)) and Vagaro's $23.99 introductory price ([Vagaro](https://www.vagaro.com/pro/pricing)), it would need 500+ customers for $10k MRR, and it carries deposit, no-show and SMS support ([screen notes](../research_notes/Solo%20developer%20SaaS%20opportunity%20shortlist/screen_group3_health_wellness_property.md)). **C05 FlatBuild** (20/25, #4 by priority) needed a full construction project-management suite before CoConstruct refugees would switch, while funded rivals JobTread and Projul were already running the same migration play; the re-screen kept only an exit-kit wedge, and validation shrank that to a one-time cash project. The **education bets** fared worst: the INDEX recommended a family-learning suite built around P03 HomeRoll, then P04 ClassHouse with P01 PhonicsPrep, then R08 Readlings, but they scored 27, 27, 27 and 21/40 because consumer prices of $49–59 a year require 700–3,000+ paying households for $10k MRR, the cheap end is crowded, and child-facing products now carry the amended COPPA rule in force since Apr 22 2026 ([screen notes](../research_notes/Solo%20developer%20SaaS%20opportunity%20shortlist/screen_group5_education_state_specific.md)). P03's 35-state priority bonus measured how widespread homeschool laws are, not how sellable the product is.

The corpus measured price gap against the named incumbent rather than the $0–30 tool a buyer finds in the same search, counted lines of code rather than the feature parity customers need before switching, rewarded huge pools one person cannot reach, and had no criterion for legal liability, cheap competitors or support load. The same lens lifted ideas the corpus left mid-pack, because they pair owner-operator buyers with dated filings and public prospect lists.

## Five build-ifs survived validation, led by a nine-day-old California law

Of eleven validated finalists, five are build-ifs, one is a cash project and five are don't-builds. The ranking weighs projected time to $5k MRR on stated assumptions, evidence that the gap is really open, and whether a dated trigger falls inside the next six months.

### 1. K02 "CARS Act 3-Day Log": build-if

California's SB 766 was chaptered Oct 6 2025 and became operative on **Oct 1 2026**; a September 2026 DMV industry memo confirms the date, and searches found no delay, amendment or lawsuit ([CalMatters Digital Democracy](https://calmatters.digitaldemocracy.org/bills/ca_202520260sb766); [DMV memo](https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/file/26olin10-pdf/)). It gives buyers of used vehicles priced at **$50,000 or less** an automatic 3-day right to cancel that ends once the car passes 400 miles, allows a restocking fee reported as **1.5% bounded at $200–$600** plus $1 per mile over 250 capped at $150 ([CBT News](https://www.cbtnews.com/?p=252530); [SlashGear](https://www.slashgear.com/2004828/california-2026-new-car-buying-law/)), obliges the dealer to return a trade-in or pay the highest of several values for it ([PolicyRisk](https://policyrisk.com/state-bill/CA-SB766-20252026)), and requires **2 years** of compliance records, not the 7 the corpus assumed ([DMV memo](https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/file/26olin10-pdf/)). Franchise dealers are served by quote-priced ComplyAuto, KPA, Tekion and Reynolds forms ([CNCDA event](https://www.cncda.org/events/carsact_complyauto/)), and SnapInspect and SecureClose each market one slice ([SnapInspect](https://blog.snapinspect.com/california-sb-766/)), but no search surfaced a CARS Act module from DealerCenter or Frazer, the dealer-management systems independent lots use, though their release notes sit behind logins. Trade press reports dealers "rush to comply" ([Dealership Guy](https://news.dealershipguy.com/p/on-top-of-ftc-rules-california-dealers-rush-to-comply-with-cars-act)).

The 4–5 week wedge sits beside the dealer's software and forms vendor and drafts no contracts. A phone **Delivery Kit** captures a VIN decode, an odometer photo, a 12–20 photo walk-around and the buyer's e-signature acknowledging condition and receipt of the cancellation form; a **3-Day Board** counts down every open window and flags trade-ins to hold; a **Return desk** checks the 400-mile limit, compares photos side by side, computes fees with the statute citation shown, and runs a 48-hour refund timer; and a **2-year binder** is searchable by VIN. Price it at **$59/mo per location or $590/yr**, with a $29 Lite plan for lots doing fewer than 15 retail deals a month. The buyer is the owner or general manager of an independent or buy-here-pay-here lot; California had **7,926 licensed used-vehicle dealers** on Jan 1 2024 ([DMV](https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/file/top-ten-california-dmv-facts-pdf)). County dealer lists cost $39–117 ([DMV fee page](https://qr.dmv.ca.gov/portal/es/?p=1560)) and carry phones and addresses but no emails, so selling means phone calls, visits along auto rows and postcards.

At $59, **$5k MRR needs 85 dealers and $10k needs 169**, about 1.1% and 2.1% of used dealers; assuming 8–15 new dealers a month after month 2 and low churn because the binder accumulates records, that is **$5k in about 6–9 months and $10k in 12–18**. The gate falls on **Fri Oct 23**: at least 5 of 10 dealers must say their software or forms vendor gives them no 3-day clock, condition proof or refund workflow and that they would pay $49–69/mo, the fee and refund numbers must be confirmed against SB 766's chaptered text, and a dealer attorney must agree to review templates; then **5 paying dealers by about Nov 24**, with founding prepayments counting. Kill it if vendors already provide the workflow or dealers report almost no cancellations by mid-November. The risks are a free 3-day module from a software or forms vendor, a one-state ceiling of roughly $55k MRR even at 10% share and $69/mo, single-state legal liability (every computed number must show its rule, be confirmed by the dealer, and state that the dealer remains responsible, as Tekion does ([Tekion](https://tekion.com/blog/preparing-for-cars-act-and-ftc-pricing-compliance-what-dealers-need-to-know))), and FTC Safeguards Rule duties for stored buyer IDs that were not researched.

### 2. B04 narrowed to "Lead Ledger": build-if

The wedge drops all telephony from v1 and ships in about 4 weeks. Each shop gets a forwarding address where AI turns Angi, Thumbtack, Google Local Services Ads (LSA), Yelp and website-form lead emails into job cards; the owner gets an alert within 10 seconds with one-tap call and text links, since person-to-person texts from the owner's own phone need no carrier registration; a booked, lost or junk tap records each job's dollar value; and a per-source profit board shows cost per lead and **cost per booked job**, with a free LSA migration kit as the lead magnet. Automated texting waits for v2 and paying users only; voice AI is out entirely. Validation found no product described as showing cost per booked job by source, the one uncontested corner of the market, though a 35-search budget could have missed one.

The trigger is Google's move of LSAs into Google Ads as Performance Max campaigns, phased from August 2026: manual cost-per-lead bidding ends and **historical performance reports do not carry over** unless exported first ([TechWyse](https://www.techwyse.com/news/platform-updates/google-local-services-ads-performance-max-migration-local-inventory-ads-default-2026)). Service-area businesses, which include most HVAC and plumbing shops, reportedly migrate in late 2026, but that date rests on a single LinkedIn post ([LinkedIn](https://ca.linkedin.com/in/rachli)). Angi averaged **106,000 monthly active pros in Q2 2026, down 17%** ([Angi Q2 2026](https://angihomeservices.gcs-web.com/static-files/10f1742d-8741-49ca-81f5-c8e8f261b3ac)), and LSA leads average $53 ([Valley Marketing Group](https://thevalleymarketinggroup.com/blog/google-local-services-ads-cost-per-lead-2026/)). The buyer is the owner of a 1–10-truck HVAC or plumbing shop spending $1k+/mo on LSA, Angi or Thumbtack and not running Housecall Pro or ServiceTitan; a commercial summary of Census data counts 111,207 plumbing and HVAC establishments ([Orbital](https://withorbital.com/data/tam-reports/hvac/)). Price it at **$49 Solo / $99 Crew**, month to month. Sell by cold email only to shops visible in LSA results in 3–4 metros, never by cold text, since plaintiffs are filing class actions over texts sent outside 8am–9pm ([Buchalter](https://www.buchalter.com/insights/one-florida-firm-is-targeting-ecommerce-brands-with-tcpa-class-actions-claiming-that-promotional-text-messages-sent-outside-of-8am-9pm-are-illegal-the-industry-is-fighting-back-and-heres-wh/)), plus posts in r/HVAC, r/Plumbing and r/sweatystartup, owner Facebook groups, and a 20–30% recurring referral fee for trade bookkeepers and coaches.

**$5k MRR needs 51–102 customers and $10k needs 101–204** (64 and 127 at a $79 blend). Assuming 1,000 cold emails a month yield 10–20 conversations and 3–5 sales, communities add 2–5 more, and churn runs 4–6% a month, that is **$5k in about 7–11 months and $10k in 14–24**. The gate is **10 shops paying for a pilot by Mon Nov 2**, 21 days after outreach starts; kill it if fewer than 3% of contacted shops engage. The risks are that owners treat source analytics as "nice to know", that a field-service suite copies the report, that lead-email formats break, that buyers already drown in GoHighLevel agency pitches, and that demand is inferred rather than observed.

### 3. F06 narrowed to "ESA Receivables Ledger": build-if

Validation reshaped this wedge. Texas schools do not send formatted invoices to Odyssey, the administrator of the state's education savings accounts (TEFA): they confirm enrollment and set tuition in Odyssey's portal, by bulk CSV if they like, the parent confirms, and Odyssey pays through Stripe ([Odyssey](https://support.withodyssey.com/hc/en-us/articles/40256443594395-Overview-Steps-Required-to-Receive-Tuition-Payments-Through-Odyssey)). Florida's Step Up For Students has the school submit an invoice in its EMA portal for parent approval, with releases on **Nov 1, Feb 1 and Apr 1** ([Step Up](https://www.stepupforstudents.org/scholarships/private-school/fund/)), and only Arizona's ClassWallet requires uploaded invoice documents ([AZ Dept of Ed](https://azed.gov/sites/default/files/2024/06/ESA%20Newsletter_June%202024.pdf)). So the invoice generator goes, and the 4–5 week product becomes a companion to FACTS or QuickBooks that processes no payments: a ledger splitting the ESA share from the parent's share, expected-versus-received tracking per installment, reminders that chase parents before each program's cut-offs, parent statements, a posting export, an audit binder and an Arizona-only invoice pack. No product doing this surfaced; FACTS and Brightwheel showed no ESA feature, and GetESAPaid, the corpus's $39/mo comparison, left no search trace and its domain did not resolve.

The scale is real. TEFA drew 274,000 applications ([FOX 7](https://www.fox7austin.com/news/texas-education-freedom-accounts-tefa-deadline-applications)), made **100,000+ awards** by Jun 11 2026 ([Community Impact](https://communityimpact.com/austin/central-austin/texas-legislature/2026/06/11/more-than-100k-students-have-been-awarded-texas-education-freedom-accounts-here-are-the-next-steps/)) and lists **~2,700 approved private schools on a public map** ([Community Impact Katy](https://communityimpact.com/katy-fulshear/education/thousands-of-katy-area-families-apply-for-new-education-accounts/)); one school with 57 TEFA students has roughly **$597k a year** in awards flowing through Odyssey ([KBTX](https://www.kbtx.com/2026/08/04/vouchers-valley-how-tefa-funds-split-private-homeschool-families/)), and a July 2026 bookkeeping guide says 30–60 day payment lags are common and recommends tracking each family's funding source ([beancount.io](https://beancount.io/es/blog/2026/07/16/starting-a-microschool-learning-pod-bookkeeping-guide)). The buyer is the business manager of an established, accredited Texas private or faith school, then Florida, then Arizona; microschools are a weaker first market, with a median of 20–22 students and only 38% taking state choice funds ([The 74](https://www.the74million.org/article/exclusive-7-things-to-know-about-microschools-in-2026/)). Price it at **$79/mo for up to 75 ESA students and $149/mo for up to 250**, with a $49 founding rate locked for the first 20 schools, and sell by cold email to business offices on the TEFA map, Catholic diocesan business offices and the Texas Private Schools Association.

**$5k MRR needs about 60 schools and $10k about 120.** One pass over 1,500 schools at an assumed 5% reply, 40% call and 30% close yields about 9, so the base case is **$5k in 9–12 months and $10k in 18–24**, which a diocesan deal could halve. The gate is **8 paid pre-orders, or 5 annual prepays, by Fri Nov 6**; drop it below 3. The decisive unknown is whether Odyssey's portal already gives schools per-student remittance reports, which would erase most of the pain, so ask prospects to share screens on discovery calls. Payment dates also conflict: Odyssey's own help page puts the last TEFA installment on Apr 1 2027 ([Odyssey timeline](https://support.withodyssey.com/hc/en-us/articles/51295974654107-TEFA-Funding-Timeline-and-Installments)) while others say Feb 1 ([Texas Policy Research](https://www.texaspolicyresearch.com/texas-education-freedom-accounts-begin-funding-students/)). Quarterly pain invites summer churn, diocesan sign-off slows sales, and which schools qualify for TEFA is politically contested ([Spectrum News](https://spectrumlocalnews.com/tx/south-texas-el-paso/news/2025/12/22/texas-comptroller-asking-to-block-private-schools-with-alleged-islamic--chinese-ties-from-funding)).

### 4. S1-01 "April 15 Filing Kit" for small New York agencies: build-if, but slow

The trigger grew stronger than the corpus claimed. The final phase of New York DFS's amended cybersecurity rule (23 NYCRR Part 500), making MFA universal and requiring written asset inventories, took effect Nov 1 2025 ([HLC](https://hlc.com/en/publications/nydfs-final-set-of-cybersecurity-requirements-under-amended-part-500-take-effect-november-1-2025)), so the **Apr 15 2027 certification is the first to cover a full year under every amended requirement**. It applies to the agency as a business, while licensed employees file 500.19(b) exemption notices instead ([Big I NY](https://www.biginy.org/?p=1982); [DFS Jul 2026 instructions](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2026/07/Submitting-or-Amending-a%20Notice-of-Exemption_1.pdf)). Enforcement is real: a **$1.9M settlement** with an insurance agency included its failure to file certifications ([Polsinelli](https://www.polsinelli.com/publications/new-york-department-of-financial-services-announces-a-1-9-million-settlement-with-insurance-agency-for-violations-of-new-yorks-cybersecurity-regulation)), and DFS fined small licensee Order Express $250k in 2026 over a weak risk assessment ([Data Protection Report](https://www.dataprotectionreport.com/2026/08/nydfs-levies-250000-fine-on-licensee-for-inadequate-cyber-risk-assessment/)). The bottom of the market is DFS's free Program Template ([DFS](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/cybersecurity/Program-Template)) and a $249 template kit ([Art of Service](https://store.theartofservice.com/ny-dfs-23-nycrr-500/)); the top is Drata, which added Part 500 support in November 2025 and costs roughly $10k+/yr ([Drata](https://drata.com/updates/drata-supports-nydfs-part-500)); nothing self-serve at $30–60/mo surfaced in between.

The pitch is "DFS's own template, done for you": an exemption wizard, the template pre-filled, an MFA and asset-inventory evidence checklist, a 20-minute staff training, a roster tracker for employees' 500.19(b) notices, a two-signer filing packet with an honest acknowledgment-of-noncompliance path, and a receipt vault. Price it at **$490/yr per agency** ($390 founding) and $149/yr for a solo producer. DFS has issued **117,497 licenses** ([NYSID](https://www.workerscompensation.com/sponsored-content/nysid-reports-32-m-in-consumer-recoveries/)), but no bulk licensee list turned up, so the channels are Big I NY and PIANY webinars, IT providers reselling at wholesale, SEO and scraped directories. **$5k MRR needs about 123 agencies and $10k about 245**; season one might bring 25–60 customers with cash up front, and $5k likely takes **18–24 months**, two filing seasons. The gate is **10 paid pre-orders, or one association or IT-provider partner, by about Nov 10**. The main risks are agencies certifying themselves with the free template and Big I NY's existing free member checklist.

### 5. B07 "HoneyBook Exit Kit": build-if, low confidence

The triggers have aged. HoneyBook's prices have not moved since February 2025 ($36/$59/$129 monthly) ([Agiled](https://agiled.app/blog/honeybook-pricing)), it **cut card fees to 2.7% + 10¢** around mid-May 2026 ([PricingSaaS](https://pricingsaas.com/companies/honeybook/diffs/2026W38)), and it **launched photographer galleries** on Jul 9 2026 ([PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2026/07/09/ai-powered-crm-platform-honeybook-launches-new-tools-for-photographers/)). More than ten tools sit at $0–60/mo, including Dubsado at $35–55 after a Dec 1 2025 price reset ([Agiled](https://agiled.app/blog/dubsado-pricing)), and "payments go to your own Stripe" no longer stands out. What remains is the migration itself: HoneyBook exports contacts, and Projects reports only on its Essentials and Premium plans, with no documented contract export ([HoneyBook Help](https://help.honeybook.com/en/articles/2209117-about-reports)), so an AI converter that turns contract PDFs into editable templates is the most convincing demo. The 5-week product adds a CSV importer, one booking link from package to e-signature to a retainer in the photographer's own Stripe account, a payment schedule and an income report. Price it at **$29/mo or $290/yr** plus a $149 done-for-you migration, and sell through photographer Facebook groups, Reddit, educator affiliates on a 30% share, and SEO into a pool of about 151,200 photographer jobs, 66% self-employed ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/media-and-communication/photographers.htm)). **$5k MRR needs 173 customers and $10k needs 345**, or 9–15 and 20–30+ months on assumed rates. The gate is **25 paid founding members, or 10 annual prepays, by Sun Nov 15**.

### C05 is a cash bridge, not a business

CoConstruct accepts new projects only through **Mar 31 2027** ([CoConstruct migration](https://coconstruct.com/migration)), but the pain is narrower than the corpus assumed: history stays viewable "indefinitely" for builders who stay with Buildertrend, the built-in export **excludes files and photos** ([CoConstruct help](https://www.coconstruct.com/learn-construction-software/how-do-i-get-a-copy-of-my-projects-data)), the API is closed to new users ([CoConstruct developers](https://coconstruct.com/developers)), JobTread, Projul and others migrate customers for free ([JobTread](https://www.jobtread.com/news/jobtread-opens-a-migration-path-for-builders-facing-the-coconstruct-phase-out)), and BuiltWith detects only 98 live CoConstruct sites ([BuiltWith](https://trends.builtwith.com/cms/Co-construct)). A converter, a per-job closeout PDF and a files-and-photos grabber (shipped only after a terms-of-service review), sold as a **$399 kit**, $1,200 done-with-you, and a $19/mo archive vault, could earn **$12k–72k in total** between November 2026 and July 2027 if it captures 1–3% of an assumed 3,000–6,000 remaining accounts, then close to nothing. Its gate is a readable export ZIP in week 1 and 5 pre-orders by day 21, and it belongs alongside the main bet as cash runway, never in place of it.

## Every number here is provisional until buyers pay

The validation round ran about 35–40 searches per finalist on Oct 10 2026, and direct page fetches were blocked by DNS errors on honeybook.com, getjobber.com, coconstruct.com, spectora.com, dfs.ny.gov, americanmedspa.org, Odyssey's support site and getesapaid.com, and by proxy 403 errors on leginfo.legislature.ca.gov and data.transportation.gov. **Most web facts in this report therefore come from search-engine summaries of the cited pages, not from reading the pages.** Many prices come from third-party directories or competitor blogs, and because Reddit was blocked for search and Facebook groups could not be searched, almost no first-person buyer complaints back any verdict. Several load-bearing facts conflict or remain unconfirmed: the final TEFA installment date (Feb 1 versus Apr 1 2027), SB 766's $200 fee floor and refund timing (48 hours versus two business days after payment is verified), when service-area businesses move to Performance Max (one LinkedIn post), Indiana's adverse-event window (5 versus 15 days), and whether GetESAPaid exists at all. Each must be checked on the primary page before it appears in a calculator or marketing copy, and nothing here is legal or tax advice.

The revenue models are planning assumptions, not data: the 1–2% positive reply rates, the 5% reply, 40% call and 30% close funnel, 3–6% monthly churn and 8–15 sales a month have no sourced benchmark behind them, and **no customer interviews have been done for any idea**, which is exactly what the gates are designed to fix. The corpus is third-party research written Sep 24–25 2026 whose own fact-check pass covered only some ideas (the K02 and K03 idea files were never fact-checked), and every rubric score, wedge and price in this report is one analyst's judgment applied to that corpus plus the Oct 10 searches.

## Conclusion

The validation round exposed a pattern the corpus could not have scored: **wedges whose value is "AI writes or calculates X" were commoditized within months**. Home-inspector report drafting now has a free InterNACHI offer, Spectora's bundled AI and at least six AI-native entrants; IFTA gained a $9/mo receipt-photo app and an AI receipt scanner in 2026; and a Delaware franchise-tax comparator was open-sourced a week before this report. The survivors share a different shape, a dated legal or payment obligation, an operational clock or reconciliation the incumbent's software does not own, and a buyer list that must be worked by hand (DMV county lists, the TEFA map, LSA listings). The same tools that make this founder fast make every rival fast, so the defensible assets are timing, distribution and the records customers accumulate, not the code.

That changes what "move fast" should mean. With building nearly cheap and selling unchanged, the founder's scarce resource is hours spent talking to buyers, so the right portfolio is many cheap pre-sale tests feeding one build, and the gate dates (Oct 23, Nov 2, Nov 6) are effectively the product strategy. When two ideas tie, prefer the one where customers keep their records with you, K02's binder or F06's ledger, because each customer costs phone calls to win and keeping them is worth more than a slightly faster funnel.
